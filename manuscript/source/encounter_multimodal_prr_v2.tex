\title{Prescribing finite-window reaction-time modes with a static fixed-budget Doi reactivity field}

\author{Xiaoxiao Zhouyi}
\email{xiaoxiao.zhouyi@bristol.ac.uk}
\affiliation{School of Engineering Mathematics and Technology, University of Bristol,
Bristol BS8 1TW, United Kingdom}

\date{\today}

\begin{abstract}
Diffusing molecules that react on contact do so at random times, and the
distribution of these reaction times can have several peaks.  We show that
a fixed amount of static catalyst, installed once in space and never
switched, can be arranged so that the reaction-time density of a diffusing
pair has exactly a prescribed number of peaks on a chosen time window.  For a
two-particle Doi process---two particles that react at a position-dependent
rate whenever they are in contact---we prove that for every fixed finite
dimension \(d\ge2\) and mode count \(m\ge1\), and every sufficiently small
budget \(B>0\), there is a nonnegative, time-independent reactivity field of
fixed integrated strength \(\int\kappa(c)\,\dd c=B\) whose reaction-time
density has exactly \(m\) nondegenerate maxima and \(m-1\) nondegenerate
minima on a declared compact positive-time window.  The construction places
\(m\) narrow catalyst slabs along the monotone mean trajectory of the pair's
centre as it relaxes in a common harmonic trap, so that each slab acts as a
separated Gaussian exposure clock; the complete peak--valley pattern of the
free clock is fixed uniformly over a compact family of positive slab weights
and transfers to the killed process for all sufficiently small \(B\).
Off-lattice simulations realize the design at moderate noise and order-one
budgets and event mass, with up to five prescribed peaks in \(d=2\) and a
two-peak check in \(d=3\); they map the noise--budget boundary beyond which
modes are lost and show that loss occurs by the approach and merger of
adjacent peaks or by depletion, the two failure directions indicated by the
proof margins, and a mean-field
hazard--survival law built from the free exposure clock reproduces that
boundary to within about \(5\%\) with no adjustable parameter.  The theorem
is an existence statement with a sequential quantifier order: it supplies
neither a budget uniform in \(d\) and \(m\) nor any statement about topology
outside the chosen window.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Many diffusion-controlled processes
\cite{smoluchowski1917,collinsKimball1949,redner2001guide,metzlerOshaninRedner2014}
are read out through the time at which a reaction first occurs: the escape
of a molecule through a small receptor patch on a cell membrane
\cite{schussSingerHolcman2007,holcmanSchuss2014narrow}, target search in the
crowded cell
\cite{condaminEtAl2007nature,benichouVoituriez2014geometry,bressloffNewby2013,grebenkovMetzlerOshanin2018pccp},
and catalysis on heterogeneous surfaces \cite{grebenkov2019heterogeneous}.
In all of these the full reaction-time distribution, not only its mean,
carries the information
\cite{mattosEtAl2012bounded,godecMetzler2016PRX,godecMetzler2016SciRep}, and
a multi-peaked distribution encodes several distinct pathways or timings.
The inverse question is a design question: given a fixed total amount of
reactivity---a catalyst load or surface coverage installed once and never
modulated in time---can the reaction-time density be made to have a
prescribed number of peaks?  This paper answers that question constructively
for a pair of diffusing particles that react at a position-dependent rate
whenever they are in contact, the Doi model
\cite{doi1976stochastic,teramotoShigesada1967,erbanChapman2009}.

Reaction-time multimodality is established rather than new.  Reaction-time
densities in confinement are typically broad and carry several time scales
\cite{brayMajumdarSchehr2013persistence,mattosEtAl2012bounded};
strategically arranged static traps can generate multimodal capture-time
densities \cite{lindsaySpoonmoreTzou2016narrow}; and distinct transport
channels, heterogeneous reaction control, and encounter geometries can create
separated time scales
\cite{godecMetzler2017twochannel,godecMetzler2016SciRep,grebenkovMetzlerOshanin2018defocusing,grebenkovMetzlerOshanin2019njp,leVotEtAl2022encounter}.
The question here is therefore not whether several peaks can occur, but whether their
complete finite-window stationary-point topology can be constructed under a
specific static resource constraint.

Several adjacent inverse-design paradigms clarify that constraint.  Classical
inverse first-passage theory uses a time-dependent absorbing boundary to
realize a prescribed survival law \cite{zuccaSacerdote2009,ekstromJanson2016inverse}.  Soft-killing
variants translate a killing profile by a time-dependent barrier and establish
existence or uniqueness for prescribed lifetime distributions
\cite{ettingerEvansHening2014prescribed,
ettingerHeningWong2020inverse,klumpKolb2024soft}.  Finite-state phase-type and
hyper-Erlang constructions generate flexible positive waiting-time laws by
introducing hidden Markov phases
\cite{asmussenNermanOlsson1996phase,thuemmlerBuchholzTelek2006phase}.
Stochastic gating modifies detection times through switching reaction states
\cite{szaboEtAl1982gated,bressloffLawley2015gated,kumarEtAl2023gated}, and kinetic proofreading
uses sequential, energy-consuming reaction steps to impose temporal delays
\cite{hopfield1974proofreading,ninio1975amplification,
mckeithan1995proofreading}.  These results provide broader distributional
control in different design spaces; they do not impose the static spatial
constraint considered here.  Linear-response theory predicts how
first-passage statistics change under small perturbations
\cite{keidarReuveni2026linear}; the question here is instead constructive:
which static field realizes a prescribed peak count.

Static spatial reactivity under a fixed total resource is likewise
established.  Heterogeneous bulk killing has been studied with fixed total
reactive strength, partially reactive network elements have been rearranged at
fixed total absorbance, and position-dependent Robin reactivity has been
optimized under a fixed integral to maximize steady flux
\cite{nguyenGrebenkov2010porous,scottEtAl2021tubular,
nicolaouMulder2023flux}.  The objective here is different: the exhaustive
transient stationary-point topology of a two-particle Doi reaction-time
density under such a constraint, together with its whole-window
positive-budget transfer.

Our admissible control is a lab-frame, time-independent, nonnegative
centre-coordinate field \(\kappa(c)\), multiplied by a fixed encounter-contact
profile and constrained by
\begin{equation}
 \int\kappa(c)\,\dd c=B.
 \label{eq:intro-budget}
\end{equation}
Every admissible allocation uses the same installed amount \(B\).  We neither
translate nor activate the field in time, introduce signed reactivity, nor add
an internal gating or proofreading state.  Under these restrictions we ask
whether a prescribed number of reaction-time modes can nevertheless be made
exact on a specified time window.  Table~\ref{tab:design-recipe} states the
resulting construction as a five-step recipe.

\noindent\textit{Main result.}---In words: for any fixed finite dimension \(d\ge2\) and any fixed finite
target number \(m\ge1\) of peaks, one can place \(m\) narrow catalyst slabs of
fixed total strength \(B\) along the path of the pair's centre so that, once
the noise-and-width scale \(\varepsilon\) is small enough and then \(B\) is
small enough, the reaction-time density on the design window has exactly
\(m\) peaks and \(m-1\) troughs; this holds for every allocation of the
total strength that gives each slab at least a fixed fraction.  The
free-exposure clock used below is the budget-rescaled density \(f_B/B\) in
the limit \(B\to0\), i.e.\ the rate at which the unkilled pair is exposed to
the catalyst.  The precise construction and quantifier order are stated in
Theorem~1 (Sec.~\ref{sec:exact-m-spine}).

The result shows that a static, non-switchable resource of fixed integrated
strength suffices to prescribe the complete finite-window peak--valley
structure of a reaction-time density, without the time-dependent barriers,
gating states, or hidden kinetic phases used by the inverse-design paradigms
above.  The upper bound of \(m\) modes for an \(m\)-component univariate
Gaussian mixture is classical
\cite{carreiraPerpinanWilliams2003modes,amendolaEngstromHaase2020modes}; the
proof burden created by the encounter process---constructing all \(2m-1\)
stationary points with uniform margins together with a global
derivative-zero bound, excluding further zeros over the whole window after
multiplication by the contact probability, and preserving the root tubes
(disjoint neighbourhoods, one around each stationary point), curvature
signs, endpoint slopes, and complement margin (a nonzero derivative bound
outside the tubes) under positive-budget Doi killing---is taken up in
Sec.~\ref{sec:exact-m-spine}, with the last transfer supplied by convergence
of the budget-rescaled density together with its first two time
derivatives, uniformly over the window and over the allocation family
(Sec.~\ref{sec:transfer}).

The theorem is an existence statement with a sequential quantifier order
(Sec.~\ref{sec:exact-m-spine}) and makes no prediction at any particular
finite parameter set.  The off-lattice campaigns of
Sec.~\ref{sec:numerics} therefore supply what it does not:
realizability at order-one budgets, the operational noise--budget
retention boundary, and the peak-merger and depletion failures in the
directions indicated by the proof margins, all judged by a classifier fixed
in advance.  Doi killing, reaction mass
balance, and spatially heterogeneous reactivity are established ingredients
\cite{doi1976stochastic,teramotoShigesada1967,erbanChapman2009,isaacsonZhang2018,isaacsonMauroNewby2016uniform,pruestelMeierSchellersheim2014area,bressloff2022feynmanKac,grebenkov2019heterogeneous,grebenkov2020paradigm}.

\paragraph{Physical picture.}
Two particles subject to the same longitudinal harmonic trap start displaced
from the trap centre and close together, and their midpoint relaxes past \(m\)
static catalyst slabs of fixed total strength \(B\).  Because the slabs are
static, the only clock is the relaxation itself: the field converts the
position of the pair into a reaction time, and the modes are the image of the
slab pattern under this map.  In the theorem regime the deterministic
relative trajectory stays inside the contact radius on the whole window and
the contact probability tends to one as \(\varepsilon\to0\) (Supplemental
Material, Lemma~S3.1), so the encounter gate is a slow positive factor and,
at leading order, the pair acts as a single reactive complex sampling the
striped hazard \(BG(t)\); the exact Doi law differs from this
time-inhomogeneous Poisson clock through the fluctuation of each walker's
exposure and its correlation with position, which Theorem~1 controls in the
small-\(B\) limit and whose net effect on the operational crossings the
simulations of Sec.~\ref{sec:numerics} measure at order-one \(B\).  A pair started at the trap centre would sample a
time-independent slab profile: the static constraint is on the reactivity,
and the transient supplies the time dependence.

Let \(q(x,t)\) be the unreacted configuration density of two mobile
particles (an encounter problem
\cite{giuggioliPerezBeckerSanders2013,leVotEtAl2022encounter}), with
conservative generator \(\mathcal L\).  Write \(r\) for the
relative coordinate and \(c\) for an encounter-centre coordinate.  For a
fixed contact profile \(\chi_a(r)\) and \(m\) normalized nonnegative
centre-space profiles \(\phi_j(c)\), \(j=1,\ldots,m\), define
\begin{align}
 \kappa_{\bm w}(c)&=B\sum_{j=1}^{m}w_j\phi_j(c),
 &w_j&\ge0,\nonumber\\
 \sum_jw_j&=1,
 &\int\phi_j(c)\,\dd c&=1,\nonumber\\
 K_{B,\bm w}(x)&=\chi_a(r)\kappa_{\bm w}(c).
 \label{eq:budget-field}
\end{align}
Every admissible allocation has the same installed centre-space resource,
\begin{equation}
 \int\kappa_{\bm w}(c)\,\dd c=B.
 \label{eq:conserved-budget}
\end{equation}
The budget is measured along the encounter-centre coordinate alone, not over
the complete two-particle configuration space: the contact profile
\(\chi_a\) is fixed and common to every design, so
Eq.~\eqref{eq:conserved-budget} fixes the installed catalyst amount and is
the constraint that every admissible allocation \(\bm w\) satisfies.  In
the construction of Sec.~\ref{sec:exact-m-spine} there is one slab per
prescribed mode.
The killed evolution, reaction-time density, and survival probability are
\begin{align}
 \partial_tq_{B,\bm w}&=(\mathcal L-K_{B,\bm w})q_{B,\bm w},
 &q_{B,\bm w}(0)&=q_0,\nonumber\\
 f_{B,\bm w}(t)&=\langle K_{B,\bm w},q_{B,\bm w}(t)\rangle,
 &-S'_{B,\bm w}(t)&=f_{B,\bm w}(t).
 \label{eq:killed-law}
\end{align}
Equation~\eqref{eq:killed-law} is the usual mass balance for a Doi
volume-reaction process \cite{doi1976stochastic}.

For longitudinal catalyst slabs, translation invariance of the common
transverse coordinate gives an exact quotient
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal Q_d^{\infty}
 =\mathbb R_z\times\mathbb R_{r_\parallel}\times
   \mathbb T_W^{d-1}.
 \label{eq:quotient}
\end{equation}
The Supplemental Material also uses the bounded counterpart
\(\mathcal Q_d^{\rm box}\).  In the construction below
(Fig.~\ref{fig:mechanism}) the midpoint \(Z_t\) moves monotonically in mean,
the relative process remains a positive distance inside the contact region
along its deterministic trajectory on the chosen window, and each normalized
slab is centred at the midpoint mean at a target time.  The
\(W^{-(d-1)}\) factor in the killing field accounts for the omitted common
transverse directions and preserves Eq.~\eqref{eq:conserved-budget}.
The Ornstein--Uhlenbeck transport with stationary initial variance is chosen
so that \(\operatorname{Var}Z_t=\varepsilon^2D_0/(2\gamma)\) is constant on
the window: the exposure clock is then a common-variance Gaussian mixture in
the monotone coordinate \(x(t)\), for which the derivative-zero count of
Sec.~\ref{sec:exact-m-spine} is exhaustive.  The single parameter
\(\varepsilon\) sets both the noise, \(D=\varepsilon^2D_0\), and the slab
width \(\varepsilon\rho\), so the mixture width \(\sigma\propto\varepsilon\)
shrinks while the mean trajectory is unchanged.

\section{Prescribed finite modality: model and theorem}
\label{sec:exact-m-spine}

The conserved-budget support construction admits encounter processes with any
prescribed \emph{fixed finite} number of reaction-time modes.  We state this
existence result in an exact quotient of a two-particle Doi process; the
construction depends on the prescribed dimension and mode count and does not
assert mode-count switching by weight redistribution on one fixed support.

Fix finite $d\ge2$, finite $m\ge1$, a transverse period $W>0$, a contact
radius $0<a<W/2$, positive scales $\ell_0,\rho>0$, and a compact interval
$I=[\tau,T]\subset(0,\infty)$.
On $\mathbb R\times\mathbb R\times\mathbb T_W^{d-1}$, consider the
independent midpoint and relative processes
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 \dd Z_t&=-\gamma(Z_t-\bar z)\,\dd t
       +\varepsilon\sqrt{D_0}\,\dd W_t,\\
 \dd R_{\parallel,t}&=-\gamma R_{\parallel,t}\,\dd t
       +2\varepsilon\sqrt{D_0}\,\dd W_{\parallel,t},\\
 \dd R_{\perp,t}&=2\varepsilon\sqrt{D_0}\,\dd W_{\perp,t}\pmod W.
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:exact-m-process}
\end{equation}

The mutually independent initial laws satisfy
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 Z_0&\sim N\!\left(z_0,\varepsilon^2D_0/(2\gamma)\right),\\
 R_{\parallel,0}&\sim N(r_{\parallel,0},\varepsilon^2u_0^2),\\
 R_{\perp,0}&\sim
 \operatorname{WN}(r_{\perp,0},\varepsilon^2\Sigma_{\perp,0}),
 \end{aligned}
 \qquad
 \begin{gathered}
 D_0,\gamma,u_0>0,\quad z_0\ne\bar z,\\
 u_0^2<4D_0/\gamma,\quad \Sigma_{\perp,0}\succ0.
 \end{gathered}
 \label{eq:exact-m-initial}
\end{equation}

Here $\operatorname{WN}$ is the wrapped Gaussian on
$\mathbb T_W^{d-1}$, $\Sigma_{\perp,0}$ is a positive-definite
$(d-1)\times(d-1)$ covariance matrix, and $|r|_{\rm mi}$ is the Euclidean norm
of the longitudinal relative coordinate together with the shortest transverse
representatives modulo $W$.

All driving Brownian motions are also independent.  Thus
$\mu(t)=\bar z+(z_0-\bar z)\e^{-\gamma t}$ is strictly monotone and
$\operatorname{Var}Z_t=\varepsilon^2D_0/(2\gamma)$: the midpoint is prepared
with its stationary variance so that only its mean relaxes, which makes every
exposure clock below have the same width $\sigma$.  The inequality
$u_0^2<4D_0/\gamma$ is used only to place the initial law in the weighted
state space of the transfer estimate in the Supplemental Material.  Under the
minimum-image
convention, assume the deterministic relative trajectory
$r_*(t)=(r_{\parallel,0}\e^{-\gamma t},r_{\perp,0})$ obeys
$\sup_{t\in I}|r_*(t)|_{\rm mi}\le a-\eta_*$ for some $\eta_*>0$.

Choose target times
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
  &\tau<t_1<\cdots<t_m<T,\qquad c_j=x(t_j),\\
  &x(t)=\frac{\operatorname{sgn}(\mu')\mu(t)}{\ell_0},
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:exact-m-centres}
\end{equation}
so that $x'(t)$ is bounded away from zero.  At the corresponding physical
centres, place normalized Gaussian catalyst slabs of width
$\varepsilon\rho$.  Their nonnegative weights belong to a compact subset of
the simplex,
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal W_{w_*}
 =\left\{\bm w:\
   \substack{\sum_{j=1}^{m}w_j=1,\\ w_j\ge w_*>0}\right\},
 \qquad 0<w_*\le\frac1m,
 \label{eq:exact-m-simplex}
\end{equation}
and define
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 \phi_{j,\varepsilon}(z)
 &=\frac{\exp[-(z-\mu(t_j))^2/(2\varepsilon^2\rho^2)]}
        {\sqrt{2\pi}\,\varepsilon\rho},\\
 K_{B,\bm w,\varepsilon}(Z,R)
 &=\bm1_{\{|R|_{\rm mi}<a\}}\frac{B}{W^{d-1}}
   \sum_{j=1}^{m}w_j\phi_{j,\varepsilon}(Z).
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:exact-m-killing}
\end{equation}

The slab normalization makes $B$ the same installed centre-space budget for
every allocation.  Set $K_{B,\bm w,\varepsilon}=B V_{\bm w,\varepsilon}$,
and let $T_0(t)$ and $q_0$ denote the unkilled semigroup and initial law.  Then
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 G_{\varepsilon,\bm w}(t)
 &=B^{-1}\mathbb E_0[K_{B,\bm w,\varepsilon}(Z_t,R_t)]
 =\langle V_{\bm w,\varepsilon},T_0(t)q_0\rangle,\\
 f_{B,\varepsilon,\bm w}(t)
 &=\mathbb E_0\!\left[K_{B,\bm w,\varepsilon}(Z_t,R_t)
  \e^{-\int_0^tK_{B,\bm w,\varepsilon}(Z_s,R_s)\,\dd s}\right].
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:exact-m-observables}
\end{equation}

When the noise scale is displayed we write $f_{B,\varepsilon,\bm w}$ for the
density $f_{B,\bm w}$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:killed-law}.
The expectation $\mathbb E_0$ is with respect to the unkilled process started
from Eq.~\eqref{eq:exact-m-initial}.  Independence gives the exact
factorization
\begin{align}
 G_{\varepsilon,\bm w}(t)
 &=a_\varepsilon(t)H_{\sigma,\bm w}(x(t)),\nonumber\\
 a_\varepsilon(t)
 &=\frac{c_{d,\varepsilon}(t)}
 {W^{d-1}\sqrt{2\pi}\,\varepsilon
  \sqrt{D_0/(2\gamma)+\rho^2}},
 \label{eq:exact-m-factorization}
\end{align}
where $c_{d,\varepsilon}(t)=
\mathbb P_0(|R_t|_{\rm mi}<a)$ and $H_{\sigma,\bm w}$ is defined below.
All dependence on $d$ enters through $c_{d,\varepsilon}$ and the
normalization $W^{-(d-1)}$.

\medskip
\noindent\textit{Theorem 1 (exact prescribed finite modality).---}
Fix finite $d\ge2$, finite $m\ge1$, the data above, and the compact window
$I$.
(i) There is $\varepsilon_0>0$ such that, for every
$0<\varepsilon<\varepsilon_0$ and every $\bm w\in\mathcal W_{w_*}$, the
unit-budget free-exposure clock $G_{\varepsilon,\bm w}$ has exactly $m$
nondegenerate interior maxima and $m-1$ nondegenerate interior minima on
$I$, ordered alternately, and no stationary endpoint.
(ii) For each such $\varepsilon$ there is a budget
$B_{\rm top}^{\rm unif}(\varepsilon)>0$ such that the budget-rescaled Doi
reaction-time density $f_{B,\varepsilon,\bm w}/B$ has the same complete
stationary signature on $I$ for every
$0<B<B_{\rm top}^{\rm unif}(\varepsilon)$ and every
$\bm w\in\mathcal W_{w_*}$.  Multiplication by $B$ leaves the stationary
points unchanged, so the conclusion also holds for the physical density
$f_{B,\varepsilon,\bm w}$ (Theorem~S3.8 of the Supplemental Material
\cite{supplementalMaterial}).

Precisely, for each fixed such $\varepsilon$ and allocation set
$\mathcal A\subseteq\mathcal W_{w_*}$, define the topology-retention budget
\begin{align}
 B_{\rm top}(\varepsilon;\mathcal A)
 &:=\sup\bigl\{b>0:\nonumber\\[-2pt]
 &\quad f_{\beta,\varepsilon,\bm w}/\beta
 \text{ has the stated}\nonumber\\[-2pt]
 &\quad\text{complete stationary signature on }I
 \nonumber\\[-2pt]
 &\quad\text{for every }\bm w\in\mathcal A
 \text{ and every }0<\beta<b\bigr\}.
 \label{eq:exact-m-btop}
\end{align}
Then
\begin{equation}
 B_{\rm top}^{\rm unif}(\varepsilon)
 :=B_{\rm top}(\varepsilon;\mathcal W_{w_*})>0
 \label{eq:exact-m-btop-unif}
\end{equation}
and (ii) holds with this value.

The mechanism can be seen without invoking a normal form.  Gaussian
convolution reduces the longitudinal part of the unit-budget exposure to
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
  H_{\sigma,\bm w}(x)
  &=\sum_{j=1}^{m}w_j
    \exp\!\left[-\frac{(x-c_j)^2}{2\sigma^2}\right],\\
  \sigma&=\frac{\varepsilon}{\ell_0}
  \sqrt{\frac{D_0}{2\gamma}+\rho^2}.
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:exact-m-mixture}
\end{equation}

Writing $q_j(x)=w_j\exp[-(x-c_j)^2/(2\sigma^2)]$,
$\pi_j(x)=q_j(x)/H_{\sigma,\bm w}(x)$, and
$L=\partial_x\log H$, let $\bar c(x)$ and
$\operatorname{Var}_{\pi(x)}(c)$ denote the mean and variance of the centres
under the finite probability vector $\bm\pi(x)$.  Then
\begin{equation}
 L(x)=\frac{\bar c(x)-x}{\sigma^2},
 \qquad
 L'(x)=\frac{\operatorname{Var}_{\pi(x)}(c)}{\sigma^4}
       -\frac{1}{\sigma^2}.
 \label{eq:exact-m-log-slope}
\end{equation}

The univariate upper bound of $m$ modes for an $m$-component Gaussian mixture
is classical \cite{carreiraPerpinanWilliams2003modes,
amendolaEngstromHaase2020modes}.  Here a direct affine--exponential proof is
retained because the Doi transfer needs all critical zeros counted with
multiplicity together with uniform root-tube, complement, curvature, and
endpoint margins.

For the adjacent pair $c_j<c_{j+1}$, the weighted crossover is
\begin{equation}
 s_j=\frac{c_j+c_{j+1}}{2}
 +\frac{\sigma^2}{c_{j+1}-c_j}
  \log\!\frac{w_j}{w_{j+1}}.
 \label{eq:exact-m-crossover}
\end{equation}

The adjacent ratio obeys
$q_{j+1}/q_j=1/9$ at
$s_j-\sigma^2\log(9)/(c_{j+1}-c_j)$ and $q_{j+1}/q_j=9$ at the corresponding
plus edge.  Thus both adjacent components remain uniformly present throughout
the crossover, while all nonadjacent components are exponentially small.
Equation~\eqref{eq:exact-m-log-slope} then yields one simple minimum at
\begin{equation}
 r_j=s_j+O(\sigma^4),
 \qquad
 L'(r_j)=\frac{(c_{j+1}-c_j)^2}{4\sigma^4}
          +O(\sigma^{-2}).
 \label{eq:exact-m-valley}
\end{equation}

Each centre similarly contributes one simple maximum at
$p_j=c_j+O(\e^{-q/\sigma^2})$ for some $q>0$ depending only on the centre
separations (Supplemental Material, Lemma~S3.4), with
$L'(p_j)=-\sigma^{-2}+o(\sigma^{-2})$.  These constructions give
$2m-1$ simple stationary points.  They are exhaustive: after multiplication
by a positive Gaussian factor, $H'$ is an exponential polynomial
\begin{equation}
 \sum_{j=1}^{m}(a_j+b_jx)\e^{\lambda_jx},
 \qquad \lambda_1<\cdots<\lambda_m,
 \label{eq:exact-m-exp-polynomial}
\end{equation}
which has at most $2m-1$ real zeros counted with multiplicity.  The extreme
affine--exponential terms dominate in the two tails, so all zeros lie in a
compact interval and real analyticity makes their number finite.  A
generalized Rolle induction then proves the bound by differentiating twice to
remove the lowest-exponent affine term.

The remaining relative-coordinate contact probability is a positive slow
factor.  Uniform bounds on its first two logarithmic time derivatives, peak
boxes of width $O(\sigma^2)$, valley boxes of width $O(\sigma^4)$, and a
sign argument on the complement of these boxes (the posterior-sector
certificate of the Supplemental Material: a sign bound on the logarithmic
slope $L$ obtained from the relative weights $\pi_j(x)$ of the slabs)
exclude any further stationary point.  Consequently the slow factor moves maxima by $O(\sigma^2)$ and
minima by $O(\sigma^4)$ without changing their number or type.  Finally, on
every fixed positive $\varepsilon$, convergence of the budget-rescaled
density together with its first two time derivatives, uniformly in the slab
weights (Sec.~\ref{sec:transfer}),
\begin{equation}
 \sup_{\bm w\in\mathcal W_{w_*}}
 \left\|\frac{f_{B,\varepsilon,\bm w}}{B}
       -G_{\varepsilon,\bm w}\right\|_{C^2(I)}
 \longrightarrow0
 \qquad (B\downarrow0)
 \label{eq:exact-m-weak-budget}
\end{equation}
preserves the disjoint neighbourhoods of the stationary points (root
tubes), their signed curvature margins, the derivative margin on their
complement, and the endpoint slopes.

The quantifier order is essential: the geometry and admissible scales may
depend on fixed $(d,m)$; one first fixes a sufficiently small
$\varepsilon>0$ and only then chooses
$B<B_{\rm top}^{\rm unif}(\varepsilon)$.  The theorem does not provide a
useful lower bound for that threshold, a uniform-in-$d$ or uniform-in-$m$
construction, an event-mass floor, or a stationary-point count outside $I$;
it also uses a whole-window contact-interior condition whose small-noise limit
is asymptotically saturated contact.  Nontrivial contact, observability at a
common positive budget, and finite-parameter robustness remain separate
numerical checks.

\subsection{Positive-budget transfer}
\label{sec:transfer}

Write \(K_{B,\bm w}=BV_{\bm w}\) and introduce interior affine control
coordinates \(\bm w(\bm\theta)=\bar{\bm w}+P\bm\theta\).  The
budget-rescaled reaction density and the free-exposure law are
\begin{equation}
 F_B(t,\bm\theta)=B^{-1}f_{B,\bm w(\bm\theta)}(t),
 \qquad
 G(t,\bm\theta)=
 \langle V_{\bm w(\bm\theta)},\e^{t\mathcal L}q_0\rangle.
 \label{eq:normalized-exposure}
\end{equation}
Here \(G\) is the reactivity \(V_{\bm w}\) averaged against the unkilled
configuration density, and the transfer rests on one observation:
\(f_{B,\bm w}=B\,G+O(B^2)\), with a remainder that stays \(O(B^2)\)
together with finitely many time and weight derivatives on any compact
window \([\tau,T]\subset(0,\infty)\), uniformly over the weights
[Eq.~\eqref{eq:mixed-jet} below].  Peaks and valleys of \(G\) are therefore
inherited by \(f_{B,\bm w}\) for all sufficiently small \(B\).
On either a bounded reflecting/periodic quotient or the reversible
natural-decay realization of Eq.~\eqref{eq:quotient}, assume \(q_0\) belongs
to the corresponding state space and \(V_{\bm w}\) is bounded on a complex
control neighbourhood of a compact interior control set.  A
Dyson expansion followed by time and control Cauchy estimates gives, for
every fixed time order \(r\), finite control multi-index \(\alpha\), and
\(0\le B\le B_{\max}\),
\begin{equation}
 \sup_{(t,\bm\theta)\in[\tau,T]\times\Theta}
 \left|
 \partial_t^r\partial_{\bm\theta}^{\alpha}(F_B-G)
 \right|
 \le C_{r,\alpha,\tau,T,\Theta}B,
 \label{eq:mixed-jet}
\end{equation}
with a constant that depends on \(B_{\max}\), the initial law, and the
profiles but not on \(B\).
The Supplemental Material accompanying this article
\cite{supplementalMaterial} gives the explicit constant and proof.  In
particular, \(C^2\) convergence on a compact positive-time window preserves
disjoint stationary-point neighbourhoods, curvature signs, endpoint slopes,
and a nonzero derivative margin on their complement.  The estimate is local
in positive time and provides neither a useful numerical value of \(B\) nor
control on the scale \(t=O(B^{-1})\).

\subsection{Three budget thresholds}
\label{sec:thresholds}

Three thresholds are kept distinct.  The theorem concerns the maximal
uniform topology-preservation threshold
\(B_{\rm top}^{\rm unif}(\varepsilon)\).  For one fixed allocation, the
Supplemental Material \cite{supplementalMaterial} derives a closed-form
sufficient value \(B_{\rm cert}(\varepsilon;\bm w,\{U_i\})\), from a term-by-term
Dyson-expansion estimate (a bound of the budget expansion in the unweighted
pairing, which avoids the weighted norm of the initial law), such
that every \(0<B<B_{\rm cert}\) preserves the free-clock signature.  Only
this sufficient value, along the fixed anchor family used in its evaluation,
has a leading-order form---valid provided that, as at both evaluated points,
the complement derivative minimum is attained at the window's left endpoint
and the first-derivative condition of the certificate is the binding
one---predicted by the endpoint-slope closed form of the Supplemental
Material and confirmed numerically at \(\varepsilon=0.1\) and \(0.05\),
\begin{equation}
 \log B_{\rm cert}
 =-\frac{c_B}{\varepsilon^2}
  +O\!\left(\log\frac1\varepsilon\right),
 \qquad c_B>0.
\label{eq:bcert-rate}
\end{equation}
Here \(\{U_i\}\) is the selected system of disjoint neighbourhoods of the
stationary points (root tubes); this
dependence is suppressed below.  The construction proves only
\(B_{\rm cert}\le B_{\rm top}(\varepsilon;\{\bm w\})\), not a bound for
the threshold uniform over the full allocation family.
High-precision, non-interval evaluations using the same relative-coordinate
initial law as the simulations give nominal values
\(\widehat B_{\rm cert}\simeq8.9\times10^{-15}\) for the \(m=2\),
\(\varepsilon=0.1\) anchor and \(2.6\times10^{-7}\) for the corresponding
\(m=3\) geometry.  By contrast, \(B_{\rm op}\) below is a finite-sample
crossing of a stated classifier: \(B_{\rm op}\simeq7.6\) and \(3.6\),
respectively.  It is not identified with either \(B_{\rm top}\) or
\(B_{\rm cert}\).
The gap measures the conservatism of the certificate rather than the reach
of the mechanism: \(B_{\rm cert}\) is obtained by comparing uniform
derivative-error bounds with curvature margins inside the root tubes and
derivative margins on their complement, including the window endpoints.  By
contrast, the fixed classifier of Sec.~\ref{sec:numerics} records the
designed mode count at budgets of order one.  At the evaluated anchors, the
dominant loss comes from the left-endpoint derivative margin (Supplemental
Material, Remark~S3.16): the certificate must preserve the sign of \(G'\)
at \(\tau\), where the first clock is \(D_L=\mu(\tau)-\mu(t_1)\) away
and \(|G'(\tau)|\) carries the Gaussian factor
\(\exp[-D_L^2/(2\ell_0^2\sigma^2)]\) with \(\sigma\) from
Eq.~\eqref{eq:exact-m-mixture}; hence
\(c_B=D_L^2/[2(D_0/(2\gamma)+\rho^2)]=0.304\) at the \(m=2\) anchor
(\(D_L=0.955\)) and \(0.132\) for the \(m=3\) geometry.  At
\(\varepsilon=0.1\) this single factor accounts for thirteen of the fifteen
decades and for about six of the seven, respectively.  Depletion does not see
the endpoint slope, so the conservatism is that of a whole-window
certificate built from a uniform absolute-error margin; sharper constants in
that margin cannot remove the endpoint factor, whereas a localized,
relative-error, or degree-theoretic sign argument could.  A general weighted-norm route is far more
conservative still (Supplemental Material, Remarks~S3.15 and S3.16).
Closing this gap analytically is open.

\label{sec:numerics}

parameters have not been placed inside the theorem's guaranteed region, so
the campaigns do not test the theorem; they test the construction at finite
parameters and probe the two loss mechanisms---separation loss and

\subsection{Method and sanity checks}
\label{sec:numerics-method}

No spatial lattice is introduced.  The midpoint, longitudinal, and
transverse coordinates of Eq.~\eqref{eq:exact-m-process} are integrated by
Euler--Maruyama steps of size \(\Delta t=10^{-3}\), and Doi killing is
realized by per-step thinning with probability
\(1-\exp(-K_{B,\bm w,\varepsilon}\,\Delta t)\) evaluated at the current
position.  All runs use deterministically seeded counter-based random
streams, one substream per walker chunk.  Two automated checks accompany
every configuration: the per-step kill probability must remain in the unit
interval (largest value \(5.6\times10^{-3}\) over the seventeen production
configurations and their \(\Delta t\)-halving twin, and \(3.1\times10^{-2}\)
over every campaign run, attained at
the \(m=2\), \(\varepsilon=0.05\), \(B=8\) threshold probe), and event counts must balance mass exactly (kills plus
survivors equal walkers).  Separately, a campaign-level zero-budget twin run
accumulates about \(10^{7}\) contact steps and produces no kills.  A
\(\Delta t\)-halving twin of the
\((m,\varepsilon,B)=(2,0.1,1)\) configuration reproduces both peak times at
bin resolution and both peak prominences to within \(0.3\%\), and the
\(d=3\) run passes a per-bin \(\Delta t\)-halving comparison with maximum
standardized bin difference \(|z_{\rm bin}|=1.67\) (difference of the two
histograms' bin fractions divided by its pooled binomial standard error).

Modality is decided by a fixed operational
classifier: reaction times are histogrammed with bin width \(0.02\) and
smoothed with a Gaussian kernel of bandwidth \(0.04\), and a local maximum
counts as a mode only if its topographic prominence (peak minus contour
base) exceeds both five covariance-aware smoothed-Poisson standard
deviations (\(\sigma_{\rm prom}\), Supplemental Material) and \(5\%\) of the maximum smoothed height on the window
\(I=[0.5,3.5]\).  Peak and contour base are linear statistics of the raw
bin counts, so the prominence variance is
\(\sum_j (A_{p,j}-A_{b,j})^2\,C_j/(N\delta)^2\), with \(A\) the kernel
smoothing weights, \(C_j\) the raw counts, \(N\) the walker count, and
\(\delta\) the bin width; a peak-only Poisson sigma, which omits the base
variance and the peak--base covariance, would only overstate the
significance ratio of counted maxima (by factors up to \(1.55\) across the
retained campaign); it was the run-time gate of the simulation driver and is
retained in every stored record, but every verdict reported here is the
covariance-aware re-evaluation of the stored counts (Supplemental Material).

Post-hoc sensitivity analysis over bandwidths
\(0.03\)--\(0.05\) and relative-prominence floors \(3\%\)--\(7\%\) leaves
the principal anchors unchanged but changes the verdict in four of the 96
phase-diagram cells adjacent to the reported boundary.  Complete method
details, configuration tables, retained histogram counts, and additional
figures are given in the Supplemental Material \cite{supplementalMaterial}.

\subsection{Mean-field hazard--survival law and the retention boundary}
\label{sec:mean-field}

The free clock also predicts where the modes are lost.  Replacing the random
exposure of a walker by its mean and neglecting the correlation between a
walker's past exposure and its present position gives the hazard--survival law
\begin{equation}
 f_1(t)=B\,G(t)\,\e^{-B\Lambda(t)},\qquad
 \Lambda(t)=\int_0^tG(s)\,\dd s,
 \label{eq:mean-field-density}
\end{equation}
which has no adjustable parameter.  Applying the fixed classifier and last-mode pass rule to bin-integrated
expected counts from \(f_1\) at nominal \(N=10^{6}\) walkers, with no
sampled trajectories, gives \(B_{\rm op}^{\rm mf}=7.36\), \(7.48\), \(>8\), \(>8\) for \(m=2\)
and \(4.29\), \(3.63\), \(1.52\), none for \(m=3\) at
\(\varepsilon=0.05\), \(0.1\), \(0.15\), \(0.2\), against the measured
crossings above; the largest relative discrepancy is \(5.3\%\) at \((2,0.05)\).  The
mean-field value \(7.36\) lies above the post-hoc bracketed midpoint span
\(6.17\)--\(7.18\), while three of the nine classifier settings remain
right-censored at \(B=8\) (Table~S4 of the Supplemental Material).  These
spans describe classifier sensitivity, not an uncertainty interval for the
fixed-protocol crossing.  The
same evaluation reproduces the classifier verdict in \(95\) of the \(96\)
phase-diagram cells [the exception, \((m,\varepsilon,B)=(3,0.075,4)\), is one
of the four classifier-sensitive cells], reproduces every counted peak time
of the production grid to within one \(0.02\) bin, and passes no budget in
the \((3,0.2)\) column.  The two loss mechanisms are explicit in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:mean-field-density} and account for the opposite
\(\varepsilon\) trends.  For \(m=2\) the late mode is lost through the
relative-prominence floor by depletion, \(\e^{-B\Lambda}\); the exposure
accumulated before the second clock decreases with \(\varepsilon\) because
the contact factor decays [\(c(\tau)\) falls from \(1.00\) at
\(\varepsilon=0.05\) to \(0.71\) at \(\varepsilon=0.2\)], so a larger budget
is needed to suppress it.  For \(m=3\) the third mode is lost as the last two clocks approach: their
spacing shrinks to \(2.3\sigma\) at \(\varepsilon=0.2\), the free-clock
prominence of the third maximum falls to \(5\%\) of the window maximum, and
a smaller depletion tilt then suffices to push it below the classifier
floor; the free-clock maximum itself persists through \(\varepsilon=0.2\)
and merges only between \(0.2\) and \(0.25\).  Equation~\eqref{eq:mean-field-density} is a
preflight estimate, not a theorem.  Its continuum residual is the exposure
fluctuation and exposure--position correlation that the exact Doi law
retains and the Poisson clock discards; the measured discrepancy also
contains sampling and time-step error, and the three contributions have not
been separated.  Their net effect shifts the retained operational crossings
by about \(5\%\) or less at these parameters.

\subsection{Robustness and failure modes}
\label{sec:numerics-robustness}

Beyond the theorem's hypotheses, a jitter campaign perturbs the slab centres
by independent Gaussian displacements of scale \(\eta s\) (with \(s\) the
minimum adjacent centre spacing) and the weights by a
\(\mathrm{Dirichlet}(50\,\bar{\bm w})\) draw, with \(50\) replicas of
\(2\times10^{5}\) walkers per point at the \(\varepsilon=0.1\), \(B=1\)
anchors (Fig.~S2 of the Supplemental Material).  The target classifier mode count survives in at least \(96\%\) of
replicas up
to \(\eta=0.15\) for both designs, decaying to \(46\%\) (\(m=2\)) and
\(40\%\) (\(m=3\)) at \(\eta=0.4\); all \(50\) weight-jitter replicas
retain the target classifier mode count for both designs.  Replica-level
classifier verdicts agree with the semi-analytic verdict of each replica's
own perturbed geometry in \(86\)--\(100\%\) of cases, but this agreement
does not by itself identify the failure mechanism, because the perturbed
free clock loses a window maximum both when adjacent slabs approach and
when a slab leaves the range \([\mu(T),\mu(\tau)]\) swept by the midpoint
mean.  A per-replica inspection of the stored geometries separates the two.
For \(m=2\), \(50\) of the \(58\) failed centre-jitter replicas have a
slab centre outside the swept range (in \(32\) the late centre lies at or
below the trap centre, which the mean never reaches) while the adjacent
spacing remains above \(3\sigma\), and only \(5\) have spacing below
\(2.3\sigma\); for \(m=3\), \(34\) of \(62\) failures have a centre
outside the swept range and \(20\) have spacing below \(2.3\sigma\) (one
replica satisfies both), while the \(9\) that satisfy neither sit at
\(2.4\)--\(4.4\sigma\), where the free clock retains three maxima but the
classified prominence falls below the floor.
No failure produced an extra peak.  Loss of the last clock past the window
end is therefore the dominant jitter failure for \(m=2\) and comparable to
spacing collapse for \(m=3\); both are geometric, and the margin
\(T-t_m\) of Table~\ref{tab:design-recipe} is the relevant one for the
former.

\subsection{Consistent dimensionalization of the numerical anchor}
\label{sec:units-mapping}

Comparison of the generator and stochastic differential equations gives the
physical single-particle diffusivity \(D=\varepsilon^2D_0\), not \(D_0\).
With length and time units \(1~\mu\mathrm m\) and \(1~\mathrm s\), the
\(\varepsilon=0.1\), \(D_0=1\) anchor therefore corresponds to
\(D=0.01~\mu\mathrm m^2/\mathrm s\).  This slow-diffusion anchor
corresponds to a confined colloid or a particle in a viscous medium.
Retaining the simulated
\(W=1\) gives a \(1~\mu\mathrm m\) transverse period and, for the
equal-weight \(m=3\), \(B=1\) case, an on-contact single-slab peak rate of
\(1.33~\mathrm s^{-1}\).  This is a dimensionalization of the simulated
point, not evidence that \(B=1\) lies below \(B_{\rm top}\); the full
mapping is given in the Supplemental Material.
In these units \(B_{\rm phys}=BL_0^d/t_0\) has the dimensions of a
\(d\)-dimensional bimolecular rate coefficient
(\(1~\mu\mathrm m^2/\mathrm s\) at \(B=1\), \(d=2\)).  The alternative anchor \(t_0=0.01~\mathrm s\)
tabulated in the Supplemental Material realizes
\(D=1~\mu\mathrm m^2/\mathrm s\), \(\gamma=100~\mathrm s^{-1}\), a
\(5\)--\(35~\mathrm{ms}\) window, and \(100~\mathrm{nm}\)-wide slabs
spaced by \(0.6\)--\(1~\mu\mathrm m\) for the \(m=3\) design at
\(\varepsilon=0.1\) (Table~S5 of the Supplemental Material); no
experimental realization is claimed.  For example, retaining the \(d=2\)
anchor geometry, the matched initial law, and the remaining parameters of
Table~S5 of the Supplemental Material, prescribe nominal clocks at \(8\),
\(16\), and \(28~\mathrm{ms}\) with particle diffusivity
\(D=1~\mu\mathrm m^2/\mathrm s\), Ornstein--Uhlenbeck relaxation rate
\(\gamma=100~\mathrm s^{-1}\), and initial midpoint mean \(4~\mu\mathrm m\)
from the trap centre.  Steps (1)--(3) of Table~\ref{tab:design-recipe} then
place equal-weight Gaussian slabs of longitudinal standard deviation
\(100~\mathrm{nm}\) at \(1.80\), \(0.81\), and \(0.24~\mu\mathrm m\) from
the centre.  The numerical free-clock check in step (4) resolves three
maxima; at \(B_{\rm phys}=100~\mu\mathrm m^2/\mathrm s\) (\(B=1\)), the
stored simulation instead places the classifier-significant peaks at
\(7.9\), \(15.7\), and \(26.3~\mathrm{ms}\), illustrating the earlier shift
of the last peak by depletion (Sec.~\ref{sec:numerics-production}).

\subsection{Scope of the numerical evidence}
\label{sec:finite-scope}

The campaigns give finite-parameter evidence that the designed signatures
can be observed at moderate noise, order-one budgets, and order-one event
mass.  They also locate failures in the anticipated directions: depletion at
large budget, approach of adjacent clocks as noise grows, a clock displaced
past the window end or spacing collapse under centre jitter, and loss of a
free-clock maximum when its separation margin closes.  These are classifier
outcomes, not exact root counts for the time-discretized process.

No spatial lattice is introduced, but the simulations retain both time-step
and sampling error.  The \(\Delta t\)-halving comparisons are consistency
checks, not a convergence theorem.  Three independent seeds agree on all 18
verdicts across six targeted configurations, and the \(m=3\) and \(m=5\)
interior anchors retain their mode counts when \(\Delta t\) is halved.
However, one independent \(\Delta t/2\) run of the boundary-adjacent
\((m,\varepsilon,B)=(3,0.175,0.5)\) cell changes from three to two classified
modes because the late candidate peak falls below both classifier
thresholds.
Since the step size also changes the random-number stream, this comparison
does not isolate time-step from sampling error; the displayed phase-grid
verdict is resolution/stream-sensitive, although the six other stored runs
of this cell at \(\Delta t=10^{-3}\) (\(5\times10^{5}\)--\(3\times10^{6}\)
walkers, five seeds) all retain three modes (Supplemental Material).
Three further independent \(10^{6}\)-walker runs at \(\Delta t/2\) also
retain three classified modes, with the late candidate at \(6.9\)--\(8.6\%\)
of the global maximum and \(9.8\)--\(12.3\) covariance-aware standard
deviations (Supplemental Material), which places the single
\(5\times10^{5}\)-walker flip as a low outlier among the stored runs of this
cell; three seeds do not, however, exclude a systematic time-step
contribution near this boundary.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The theorem connects spatial first-passage theory with inverse timing
design: a static, non-switchable catalyst budget of fixed integrated strength
suffices to prescribe the complete finite-window peak--valley structure of a
reaction-time density, a control that the inverse-design paradigms of
Sec.~\ref{sec:introduction} obtain with time-dependent barriers, gating, or
hidden kinetic phases.  Relative to strategically arranged absorbing traps,
it provides an arbitrary prescribed fixed finite mode count, an exhaustive
maxima--minima count on a declared window, uniformity over a compact family
of positive allocations, and transfer to a two-particle positive-budget Doi
law under a conserved centre-coordinate resource.  Relative to inverse
first-passage constructions, it uses a static lab-frame field but prescribes
only finite-window topology rather than an arbitrary lifetime distribution.
Relative to phase-type, gated, and proofreading mechanisms, it introduces no
hidden reaction phase or time-dependent switch.  The novelty is this
conjunction of constraints, not multimodality by itself.

Mathematically, stationary catalyst locations become separated exposure
clocks.  The affine--exponential zero bound
[Eq.~\eqref{eq:exact-m-exp-polynomial}] makes the Gaussian-clock count
exhaustive; root-tube and posterior-sector estimates retain a derivative
margin on the entire complement; the contact factor perturbs rather than
creates the clock signature; and compact-positive-time \(C^2\) convergence
transfers all maxima, minima, and curvature signs to the killed law.
The transfer itself is perturbative in \(B\) at fixed \(\varepsilon\); the
substantive content is the whole-window certificate with margins uniform
over the allocation family and the explicit, weighted-norm-free sufficient
budget \(B_{\rm cert}\), neither of which follows from \(C^2\) continuity
alone.
Uniformity over \(w_j\ge w_*\) is important: the conclusion is not tied to one
finely tuned allocation, although changing weights on the same support is not
claimed to switch among arbitrary mode counts.
The budget itself enters the theorem only as the perturbation scale:
\(K_{B,\bm w}=BV_{\bm w}\) is linear in \(B\) and Theorem~1 is a
small-\(B\) statement, so the fixed-\(L^1\) constraint defines the
comparison class of allocations rather than an obstruction that the proof
overcomes; its content lies in the uniformity over that class and, numerically,
in the fixed-\(B\) comparisons of allocation pairs and weight jitter in
Sec.~\ref{sec:numerics}.

The simulations provide finite-parameter evidence rather than an additional
theorem.  At the scanned parameters they recover the designed classifier mode
counts, locate operational mode-retention boundaries, and show failure
through the same two mechanisms indicated by the proof margins: loss of a
clock's separation margin---adjacent-clock approach as noise grows or,
under geometric jitter, a clock displaced past the window end or adjacent
spacing collapse---and suppression of a late mode by depletion as the budget
grows.  Both mechanisms, and the
operational boundary itself, are reproduced without adjustable parameters by
the mean-field hazard--survival law \(f_1=BG\,\e^{-B\Lambda}\) built from
the free exposure clock, to within about \(5\%\) in the retained crossings and one
bin in the counted peak times.  These are comparisons of finite-sample
classifier outputs, not bounds on continuum error: the remaining
discrepancy combines the exposure fluctuation and exposure--position
correlation that the Poisson clock discards---which Theorem~1 controls
only in the small-\(B\) limit---with sampling and time-step error, and
their separate contributions have not been resolved.  The semi-analytic free clock is
consequently a quantitative preflight diagnostic, but the finite-walker
classifier, seed, bandwidth, prominence rule, and time step remain part of
the operational definition near a boundary.

The restrictions are substantive.  First, the deterministic relative
trajectory lies strictly inside the contact region and the free contact
probability approaches one in the small-noise limit.  The modal skeleton is
therefore generated primarily by separated longitudinal exposure clocks, not
by a nontrivial contact-gating effect.
The centre-coordinate dependence of \(K\) is itself an idealization: at the
\(\varepsilon=0.1\) anchor the slab width \(\varepsilon\rho=0.1\) is smaller
than the contact radius \(a=0.4\) and comparable to the stationary
longitudinal separation \(\varepsilon\sqrt{2D_0/\gamma}=0.14\), so a static
field acting on the position of one particle rather than on the pair centre
would convolve each clock with the relative law.  Along the deterministic
relative trajectory the resulting shift, \(|r_*|/2\le0.03\) on \(I\), is
well below the slab width, and the added variance
\(\operatorname{Var}R_{\parallel}/4\le\varepsilon^2D_0/(2\gamma)\) would
enlarge \(\sigma\) by up to about \(15\%\) at this anchor (an ungated
estimate; conditioning on contact narrows the relative law and reduces the
broadening); a single-particle field
is therefore a natural finite-parameter test beyond the theorem, not part of
it.
Second, the extension to \(d\ge2\)
uses longitudinal slabs and a transverse symmetry quotient; it demonstrates
dimension compatibility rather than a new dimension-driven geometry.
Third, the normalized slab height scales as \(1/\varepsilon\), so the
fixed-\(L^1\) constraint imposes neither an independent peak-rate cap nor a
minimum feature size; the supremum of the field,
\(B\max_jw_j/(\sqrt{2\pi}\,\varepsilon\rho W^{d-1})\) for well-separated
slabs, is small in the theorem regime only because \(B\) is taken small at
fixed \(\varepsilon\), and an additional \(L^\infty\) cap is binding only at
finite parameters (on-contact peak rate \(1.33\) at the \(m=3\), \(B=1\)
anchor).  Fourth, \(B_{\rm top}\) is a topology threshold,
\(B_{\rm cert}\) is only a fixed-allocation sufficient value, and
\(B_{\rm op}\) is a classifier-dependent finite-sample crossing; none is
identified with another.

Nothing is proved about topology outside \(I\), a parameter choice uniform in
\(d\) or \(m\), a common positive event-mass floor, or mode-count switching on
one fixed support family.  Natural next questions are whether prescribed
modality persists at a prescribed nonvanishing budget under simultaneous
\(L^1\) and \(L^\infty\) constraints, under a minimum catalyst width, or when the contact probability remains
genuinely nontrivial throughout the design window.  Within the stated scope,
the result gives a constructive topology theorem for a static
resource-constrained Doi field and a finite-parameter test of its mechanism.
