\subsection{A fixed-allocation sufficient budget}
\label{subsec:exactmfull-b0}

Let \(B_{\rm top}(\varepsilon;\mathcal A)\) denote the connected
topology-retention threshold for an allocation set \(\mathcal A\): the
supremum of all \(b>0\) for which the complete stationary signature holds for
every allocation in \(\mathcal A\) and every budget in \((0,b)\).
Theorem~\ref{thm:exactmfull-doi} proves
\(B_{\rm top}(\varepsilon;\mathcal W_{w_*})>0\) but obtains it by compactness.
This subsection instead fixes one interior allocation \(w\) and constructs a
computable sufficient budget \(B_{\rm cert}(\varepsilon;w,\{U_i\})\).  It
satisfies
\begin{equation}
 B_{\rm cert}(\varepsilon;w,\{U_i\})
 \le B_{\rm top}(\varepsilon;\{w\}),
 \label{eq:exactmfull-bcert-relation}
\end{equation}
but is not a bound for the threshold uniform over a larger allocation family
unless all constants are made uniform over that family.  It must also not be
identified with the operational finite-sample classifier crossing
\(B_{\rm op}\) reported in the numerical campaign.  Two
quantitative routes are available.  The route through
Theorem~\ref{thm:mixed-jet} --- Cauchy--Schwarz in the weighted space
\(X_\pi=L^2(\pi^{-1}\dd x)\) --- is completely general but pays the weighted
norm \(\|q_0\|_X\sim\e^{\gamma(z_0-\bar z)^2/(\varepsilon^2D_0)}\), which
destroys the numerical value of the resulting threshold; it is retained for
comparison as Remark~\ref{rem:exactmfull-b0-baseline}.  The main result,
Proposition~\ref{prop:exactmfull-b0}, avoids the weighted norm altogether:
it bounds the observable remainder by a Dyson expansion in the flat
(forward, probabilistic) pairing, estimated leg by leg on the explicit
complex-time Mehler kernels of the slab design, and recovers the two time
derivatives required by the signature argument through a Cauchy estimate on
disks of \(\varepsilon\)-scaled radius \(r_0\).  Every constant on the flat
Dyson-transfer side of the sharpened threshold is explicit and grows at most
polynomially in \(\varepsilon^{-1}\); the signature margins themselves may be
exponentially small.  No weighted-norm exponential is introduced: the price is
the radius \(r_0\propto\varepsilon\) in place of \(\tau/2\) --- a
polynomial-in-\(\varepsilon\) Cauchy cost rather than a weighted-norm
exponential.  The sharpened route uses
the product Ornstein--Uhlenbeck/Ornstein--Uhlenbeck/torus structure of the
free process, the Gaussian slabs with the compactly supported contact
indicator, and the Gaussian initial law
[Eqs.~\eqref{eq:exactmfull-midpoint-sde}--\eqref{eq:exactmfull-killing}];
it is a proposition about this slab design, not a replacement for the
abstract Theorem~\ref{thm:mixed-jet}.
Remark~\ref{rem:exactmfull-b0-anchor} evaluates both routes at a worked
anchor and at two secondary parameter sets, using the same relative-coordinate
initial law as the off-lattice campaign.  The comparison with \(B_{\rm op}\)
is diagnostic only.

\emph{Setting and notation.}
Throughout this subsection the data of Theorem~\ref{thm:exactmfull-doi} are
fixed on the unbounded natural-decay quotient \(\Qinf\) of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:supp-domains}, with the allocation family the singleton
\(\mathcal W=\{w\}\) for one fixed interior allocation \(w\) (the pointwise
case recorded in the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:exactmfull-doi}).  We write
\(\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle\) for the flat (Lebesgue) pairing of a bounded
function against an integrable density on \(\Qinf\), so that the
free-exposure clock and the budget-rescaled Doi density of
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:exactmfull-factorization} and
\eqref{eq:exactmfull-rescaled-density} are
\(G(t)=\langle V_{w,\varepsilon},\e^{t\Lfree}q_0\rangle\) and
\(\mathcal F(t)=\mathcal F_{B,\varepsilon,w}(t)
=\langle V_{w,\varepsilon},\e^{t(\Lfree-BV_{w,\varepsilon})}q_0\rangle\)
[Eq.~\eqref{eq:supp-state}].  Put
\(v_\infty=\|V_{w,\varepsilon}\|_\infty\) and define the observable
remainder at complex time,
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal R_B(z)
 =\bigl\langle V_{w,\varepsilon},
 \bigl(\e^{z\Lfree}-\e^{z(\Lfree-BV_{w,\varepsilon})}\bigr)q_0
 \bigr\rangle,
 \qquad \operatorname{Re}z>0,
 \label{eq:exactmfull-b0-remainder}
\end{equation}
so that \(\mathcal R_B(t)=G(t)-\mathcal F(t)\) for real \(t>0\).

\emph{Why the weighted norm appears, and how it is avoided.}
The route of Theorem~\ref{thm:mixed-jet} estimates \(\mathcal R_B\) by an
operator bound plus Cauchy--Schwarz in \(X_\pi\)
[Eqs.~\eqref{eq:supp-operator-bound}--\eqref{eq:supp-remainder}]; since
\(q_0\) is centred at \(z_0\ne\bar z\) while \(\pi\) is centred at \(\bar z\)
with variance \(O(\varepsilon^2)\), the factor \(\|q_0\|_X\) is
exponentially large in \(1/\varepsilon^2\).  The weighted pairing is forced
there because the complex-time free semigroup is a bounded analytic family
only in the self-adjoint representation: on flat Lebesgue spaces the
complex-time Ornstein--Uhlenbeck semigroup is not a bounded operator family
(the complexified drift displaces mass by an imaginary amount proportional
to the starting point, and the flat operator norm diverges).  The repair
rests on two observations.  First, at real time no weighted norm is needed
at all: by Feynman--Kac [Eq.~\eqref{eq:exactmfull-reaction-density}] and
\(0\le\int_0^tV(X_s)\,\dd s\le v_\infty t\) pathwise,
\(0\le G(t)-\mathcal F(t)\le(\e^{Bv_\infty t}-1)G(t)\); the whole difficulty
is the two \emph{time derivatives} required by the signature argument.
Second, derivatives can be recovered by Cauchy's estimate on disks
\(|z-t|\le r_0\) with \(r_0\propto\varepsilon\), because every leg of a flat
Dyson chain starts either from the initial law or from a potential node,
and both confine the leg's starting point to an \(O(\varepsilon)\)
neighbourhood of an \(O(1)\) centre; with \(|\operatorname{Im}z|\le
r_0=O(\varepsilon)\), the imaginary-displacement penalties are linear in the
leg durations and total \(O(1)\) per chain, uniformly in the chain order
(Lemma~\ref{lem:exactmfull-b0-legs}).  No operator bound on a flat complex
semigroup is ever used: the weighted-space theory of
Proposition~\ref{prop:supp-analytic} enters only \emph{qualitatively}, to
guarantee that \(z\mapsto\mathcal R_B(z)\) is analytic and that its Dyson
series converges, while every quantitative estimate is performed on
explicit finite-dimensional complex Gaussian integrals
(Proposition~\ref{prop:exactmfull-b0-chain}).

\emph{Kernels.}
For \(\upsilon\in\mathbb C\) with \(\operatorname{Re}\upsilon>0\) write
\(\mathcal N(u;\upsilon)=\e^{-u^2/(2\upsilon)}/\sqrt{2\pi\upsilon}\)
(principal square root).  Over a real duration \(u>0\) the free transition
density factorizes over the coordinate blocks of
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:exactmfull-midpoint-sde}--%
\eqref{eq:exactmfull-relative-perp-sde}: started at
\(y=(y_Z,y_\parallel,y_\perp)\),
\begin{equation}
 \begin{split}
 p_u(x\,|\,y)
 ={}&\mathcal N\!\bigl(x_Z-\bar z-(y_Z-\bar z)\e^{-\gamma u};
   \varepsilon^2v_Z(u)\bigr)\,
  \mathcal N\!\bigl(x_\parallel-y_\parallel\e^{-\gamma u};
   \varepsilon^2v_\parallel(u)\bigr)\\
 &\times
  \sum_{k\in\mathbb Z^{d-1}}\,
  \prod_{i=1}^{d-1}
  \mathcal N\!\bigl(x_{\perp,i}-y_{\perp,i}+k_iW;
   \varepsilon^2v_\perp(u)\bigr),
 \end{split}
 \label{eq:exactmfull-b0-kernels}
\end{equation}
where the lattice sum wraps the transverse block onto the torus and
\begin{equation}
 v_Z(\zeta)=\frac{D_0}{2\gamma}\bigl(1-\e^{-2\gamma\zeta}\bigr),
 \qquad
 v_\parallel(\zeta)=\frac{2D_0}{\gamma}\bigl(1-\e^{-2\gamma\zeta}\bigr),
 \qquad
 v_\perp(\zeta)=4D_0\zeta,
 \label{eq:exactmfull-b0-blockvar}
\end{equation}
consistent with Eqs.~\eqref{eq:exactmfull-stationary-variance} and
\eqref{eq:exactmfull-relative-covariance}.  The mean multipliers
\(\e^{-\gamma\zeta}\) (blocks \(Z\), \(\parallel\)) and \(1\) (block
\(\perp\)) and the variances \eqref{eq:exactmfull-b0-blockvar} are entire in
the duration; their values at \(\zeta=zh\), \(\operatorname{Re}z>0\),
\(h\in(0,1]\), define the \emph{complex-leg kernels} used below (well
defined by Lemma~\ref{lem:exactmfull-b0-legs}(a)).

\begin{lemma}[Complex Gaussian domination]
\label{lem:exactmfull-b0-domination}
Let \(\upsilon\in\mathbb C\) with \(\operatorname{Re}\upsilon>0\), write
\(\vartheta=\arg\upsilon\in(-\pi/2,\pi/2)\) and
\(1/(2\upsilon)=\mathfrak a-\mathrm i\mathfrak b\), so that
\(\mathfrak a=\cos\vartheta/(2|\upsilon|)>0\) and
\(\mathfrak b=\sin\vartheta/(2|\upsilon|)\).  Let
\(\mathfrak m=\mathfrak m_R+\mathrm i\mathfrak m_I\in\mathbb C\).  Then for
every \(\lambda\in(0,1)\) and all \(x\in\mathbb R\),
\begin{equation}
 \bigl|\mathcal N(x-\mathfrak m;\upsilon)\bigr|
 \le\kappa(\lambda,\vartheta)\,
 g_{s^2}(x-\mathfrak m_R)\,
 \exp\!\bigl(P_\lambda\,\mathfrak m_I^2\bigr),
 \label{eq:exactmfull-b0-domination}
\end{equation}
where \(g_{s^2}\) is the unit-mass real Gaussian density of variance
\(s^2=1/(2\mathfrak a(1-\lambda))\) (this variance symbol is unrelated to
the simplex variables \(s_k\) of the Dyson expansion) and
\begin{equation}
 \kappa(\lambda,\vartheta)
 =\bigl[(1-\lambda)\cos\vartheta\bigr]^{-1/2},
 \qquad
 P_\lambda
 =\mathfrak a+\frac{\mathfrak b^2}{\lambda\mathfrak a}
 =\Bigl(1+\frac{\tan^2\vartheta}{\lambda}\Bigr)
  \frac{\cos^2\vartheta}{2\operatorname{Re}\upsilon}
 \le\Bigl(1+\frac{\tan^2\vartheta}{\lambda}\Bigr)
  \frac{1}{2\operatorname{Re}\upsilon}.
 \label{eq:exactmfull-b0-kappaP}
\end{equation}
If \(\mathfrak m_I=0\), the bound holds with \(\lambda=0\),
\(\kappa=(\cos\vartheta)^{-1/2}\), and no penalty factor.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\(|\mathcal N(x-\mathfrak m;\upsilon)|
=|2\pi\upsilon|^{-1/2}\exp\{-\operatorname{Re}[(x-\mathfrak m)^2/(2\upsilon)]\}\).
With \(\xi=x-\mathfrak m_R\),
\((x-\mathfrak m)^2=\xi^2-\mathfrak m_I^2-2\mathrm i\xi\mathfrak m_I\),
hence
\(\operatorname{Re}[(x-\mathfrak m)^2(\mathfrak a-\mathrm i\mathfrak b)]
=\mathfrak a\xi^2-\mathfrak a\mathfrak m_I^2
-2\mathfrak b\mathfrak m_I\xi\) and
\begin{equation*}
 |\mathcal N|
 =|2\pi\upsilon|^{-1/2}
 \exp\!\bigl(-\mathfrak a\xi^2+2\mathfrak b\mathfrak m_I\xi
 +\mathfrak a\mathfrak m_I^2\bigr).
\end{equation*}
The cross term carries no useful sign --- \(\mathfrak b\) and
\(\mathfrak m_I\) may each take either sign --- so it is bounded in absolute
value by Young's inequality,
\(|2\mathfrak b\mathfrak m_I\xi|\le\lambda\mathfrak a\xi^2
+\mathfrak b^2\mathfrak m_I^2/(\lambda\mathfrak a)\), giving
\(|\mathcal N|\le|2\pi\upsilon|^{-1/2}
\e^{-(1-\lambda)\mathfrak a\xi^2}\e^{P_\lambda\mathfrak m_I^2}\).
The Gaussian factor equals \(\sqrt{2\pi s^2}\,g_{s^2}(\xi)\) with
\(s^2=1/(2\mathfrak a(1-\lambda))\), and
\((2\pi s^2/|2\pi\upsilon|)^{1/2}
=[2\mathfrak a(1-\lambda)|\upsilon|]^{-1/2}
=[(1-\lambda)\cos\vartheta]^{-1/2}\)
by \(\mathfrak a=\cos\vartheta/(2|\upsilon|)\).  The alternative forms of
\(P_\lambda\) follow from \(\mathfrak b/\mathfrak a=\tan\vartheta\) and
\(\operatorname{Re}\upsilon=|\upsilon|\cos\vartheta\).  For
\(\mathfrak m_I=0\) the cross term vanishes and no Young step is needed.
\end{proof}

Only three consequences of Lemma~\ref{lem:exactmfull-b0-domination} are used
below: the dominating Gaussian has unit mass (its variance is irrelevant);
the penalty factor depends on the leg's starting point only through
\(\mathfrak m_I^2\); and the final bound in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:exactmfull-b0-kappaP} applies with
\(\upsilon=\varepsilon^2v(\zeta)\).  For the wrapped transverse kernel the
image sum in Eq.~\eqref{eq:exactmfull-b0-kernels} is dominated image by
image (there \(\mathfrak m_I=0\)), and the dominating image sum has unit
mass on any period cell.

\begin{lemma}[Complex-leg kernel data]
\label{lem:exactmfull-b0-legs}
Let \(h\in(0,1]\) and \(z\in\mathbb C\) with \(u=\operatorname{Re}z>0\), and
set \(\zeta=zh\), \(A=2\gamma hu\ge0\).  Then, for the three block variances
of Eq.~\eqref{eq:exactmfull-b0-blockvar}:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] \(\operatorname{Re}v(\zeta)>0\); specifically
\(\operatorname{Re}v_Z(\zeta)\ge D_0hu\,\e^{-A}\),
\(\operatorname{Re}v_\parallel(\zeta)\ge4D_0hu\,\e^{-A}\), and
\(\operatorname{Re}v_\perp(\zeta)=4D_0hu\).
\item[(b)] \(|\tan\arg v(\zeta)|\le|\operatorname{Im}z|/u\) for all three
blocks.
\item[(c)] The mean multipliers obey
\((\operatorname{Im}\e^{-\gamma\zeta})^2
\le\gamma^2h^2(\operatorname{Im}z)^2\e^{-A}\) for the \(Z\) and
\(\parallel\) blocks; the \(\perp\) multiplier is \(1\).
\item[(d)] If additionally \(|\operatorname{Im}z|\le r_0\) and
\(u\ge\tau-r_0\) for some \(0<r_0<\tau\), then, after
Lemma~\ref{lem:exactmfull-b0-domination} with parameter
\(\lambda\in(0,1)\) is applied blockwise, a \(Z\)-block leg of duration
\(\zeta\) started at \(y_Z\) carries the penalty factor
\(\exp[\omega_Z(h)(y_Z-\bar z)^2]\) and a \(\parallel\)-block leg started at
\(y_\parallel\) carries \(\exp[\omega_\parallel(h)y_\parallel^2]\), with
\begin{equation}
 \begin{gathered}
 \omega_Z(h)\le\Omega_Zh,
 \qquad
 \omega_\parallel(h)\le\Omega_\parallel h,
 \qquad
 t_\vartheta=\frac{r_0}{\tau-r_0},\\
 \Omega_Z
 =\Bigl(1+\frac{t_\vartheta^2}{\lambda}\Bigr)
  \frac{\gamma^2r_0^2}{2\varepsilon^2D_0(\tau-r_0)},
 \qquad
 \Omega_\parallel=\frac{\Omega_Z}{4}.
 \end{gathered}
 \label{eq:exactmfull-b0-budget}
\end{equation}
Both bounds are \emph{linear} in the leg fraction \(h\); along any chain
whose leg fractions sum to one, the penalty coefficients therefore total at
most \(\Omega_Z\) (respectively \(\Omega_\parallel\)), independently of the
chain order and of the leg configuration.  The \(\perp\) blocks carry no
penalty.
\item[(e)] Under the same conditions, the domination constant of one leg
(the product over the \(d+1\) scalar blocks, the Young step applied only to
the \(Z\) and \(\parallel\) blocks) is at most
\begin{equation}
 \hat\kappa
 =(1-\lambda)^{-1}\bigl(1+t_\vartheta^2\bigr)^{(d+1)/4}.
 \label{eq:exactmfull-b0-kappahat}
\end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
(a) \(\operatorname{Re}(1-\e^{-2\gamma\zeta})
=1-\e^{-A}\cos(2\gamma h\operatorname{Im}z)\ge1-\e^{-A}\ge A\e^{-A}\);
multiplying by the positive prefactors in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:exactmfull-b0-blockvar} gives the two
Ornstein--Uhlenbeck bounds, and
\(\operatorname{Re}v_\perp(\zeta)=4D_0h\operatorname{Re}z\) is exact.

(b) For the \(\perp\) block, \(\arg v_\perp(\zeta)=\arg z\) and
\(|\tan\arg z|=|\operatorname{Im}z|/u\).  For the Ornstein--Uhlenbeck
blocks,
\(|\operatorname{Im}(1-\e^{-2\gamma\zeta})|
=\e^{-A}|\sin(2\gamma h\operatorname{Im}z)|
\le\e^{-A}\,2\gamma h|\operatorname{Im}z|
=(|\operatorname{Im}z|/u)A\e^{-A}\); dividing by the real-part lower bound
in (a) gives the claim.

(c) \((\operatorname{Im}\e^{-\gamma\zeta})^2
=\e^{-2\gamma hu}\sin^2(\gamma h\operatorname{Im}z)
\le\e^{-A}\gamma^2h^2(\operatorname{Im}z)^2\).

(d) The \(Z\)-block leg kernel is
\(\mathcal N(x_Z-\mathfrak m;\varepsilon^2v_Z(\zeta))\) with
\(\mathfrak m=\bar z+(y_Z-\bar z)\e^{-\gamma\zeta}\), so
\(\mathfrak m_I^2=(y_Z-\bar z)^2(\operatorname{Im}\e^{-\gamma\zeta})^2\).
By (b), \(|\tan\arg v_Z(\zeta)|\le|\operatorname{Im}z|/u\le t_\vartheta\),
so Eq.~\eqref{eq:exactmfull-b0-kappaP} and (a), (c) give the penalty
exponent
\begin{equation*}
 P_\lambda\mathfrak m_I^2
 \le\Bigl(1+\frac{t_\vartheta^2}{\lambda}\Bigr)
 \frac{\gamma^2h^2(\operatorname{Im}z)^2\e^{-A}}
      {2\varepsilon^2D_0hu\,\e^{-A}}\,(y_Z-\bar z)^2
 =\Bigl(1+\frac{t_\vartheta^2}{\lambda}\Bigr)
 \frac{\gamma^2h(\operatorname{Im}z)^2}{2\varepsilon^2D_0u}\,
 (y_Z-\bar z)^2
 \le\Omega_Zh\,(y_Z-\bar z)^2:
\end{equation*}
the factors \(\e^{-A}\) cancel exactly, and
\((\operatorname{Im}z)^2\le r_0^2\), \(u\ge\tau-r_0\) were used in the last
step.  The \(\parallel\) block is identical with \(4D_0\) in place of
\(D_0\) and mean \(y_\parallel\e^{-\gamma\zeta}\), which yields the extra
factor \(1/4\).  The \(\perp\) blocks have \(\mathfrak m_I=0\) by (c) and
the final sentence of Lemma~\ref{lem:exactmfull-b0-domination}.  Linearity
in \(h\) and \(\sum_kh_k=1\) give the budget statement.

(e) The \(Z\) and \(\parallel\) blocks contribute
\([(1-\lambda)\cos\vartheta]^{-1/2}\) each and the \(d-1\) transverse
blocks \((\cos\vartheta)^{-1/2}\) each, with
\(|\tan\vartheta|\le t_\vartheta\) throughout by (b), so
\(\cos\vartheta\ge(1+t_\vartheta^2)^{-1/2}\) and the product is at most
\((1-\lambda)^{-1}(\cos\vartheta)^{-(d+1)/2}
\le(1-\lambda)^{-1}(1+t_\vartheta^2)^{(d+1)/4}\).
\end{proof}

After Lemma~\ref{lem:exactmfull-b0-domination} has been applied to every
leg of a chain, the surviving dependence on a chain node \(y\) is the
factor
\(\exp[\nu(y_Z-\bar z)^2+\nu'y_\parallel^2]\)
multiplying \(V_{w,\varepsilon}(y)\) at interior nodes or \(q_0(y)\) at the
starting point.  The next lemma absorbs these factors by exact Gaussian
algebra.

\begin{lemma}[Penalty absorption by complete squares]
\label{lem:exactmfull-b0-absorption}
Write \(\phi_w=\sum_jw_j\phi_{j,\varepsilon}\) for the slab mixture of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:exactmfull-slabs}, \(\mu_j=\mu(t_j)\) for its physical centres
(distinct from the dimensionless \(c_j=x(t_j)\) of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:exactmfull-targets}),
and note \(\sup_y\phi_w=W^{d-1}v_\infty\)
[Eq.~\eqref{eq:exactmfull-killing}; the contact indicator attains
\(\sup\chi_a=1\)].
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] \emph{(Weighted slab-mixture supremum.)}
Let \(\nu\ge0\) with \(x=2\varepsilon^2\rho^2\nu<1\).  For each
component the exact complete-square identity
\begin{equation}
 \begin{gathered}
 \mathcal N(y-\mu_j;\varepsilon^2\rho^2)\,
 \e^{\nu(y-\bar z)^2}
 =(1-x)^{-1/2}
 \exp\!\left[\frac{\nu(\mu_j-\bar z)^2}{1-x}\right]
 \mathcal N\!\left(y-\mu_j(x);\frac{\varepsilon^2\rho^2}{1-x}\right),\\
 \mu_j(x)=\frac{\mu_j-x\bar z}{1-x},
 \end{gathered}
 \label{eq:exactmfull-b0-square}
\end{equation}
holds.  Consequently, with
\(\hat c=\max_j|\mu_j-\bar z|\),
\begin{equation}
 \begin{gathered}
 \sup_y\bigl[\phi_w(y)\,\e^{\nu(y-\bar z)^2}\bigr]
 \le(1-x)^{-1/2}
 \exp\!\left[\frac{\nu\hat c^{\,2}}{1-x}\right]
 S_w(x),\\
 S_w(x)=\sup_y\sum_{j=1}^m w_j\,
 \mathcal N\!\left(y-\mu_j(x);\frac{\varepsilon^2\rho^2}{1-x}\right):
 \end{gathered}
 \label{eq:exactmfull-b0-Sw}
\end{equation}
\(S_w(x)\) is the supremum of the same mixture with every component width
inflated by \((1-x)^{-1/2}\) and every centre moved outward from \(\bar z\)
by the map \(\mu_j\mapsto\mu_j(x)\).  With \(x_V=2\varepsilon^2\rho^2\Omega_Z\)
(an upper bound for every node argument occurring below), define the
inflation slack
\begin{equation}
 \delta_v
 =\max\Bigl\{0,\;
 \sup_{0\le x\le x_V}\frac{S_w(x)}{W^{d-1}v_\infty}-1\Bigr\}
 \;\ge\;0 .
 \label{eq:exactmfull-b0-deltav}
\end{equation}
(At all three evaluation points of Remark~\ref{rem:exactmfull-b0-anchor},
the floating-point search gives \(\widehat\delta_v=0\): the width inflation
lowers the sampled mixture supremum.  This observation is not an interval
enclosure of the exact supremum.)
\item[(b)] \emph{(Contact node.)}  On the support of \(\chi_a\),
\(|y_\parallel|\le|y|_{\rm mi}<a\), so
\(\e^{\nu'y_\parallel^2}\le\e^{\nu'a^2}\) there; the transverse
blocks carry no penalty.
\item[(c)] \emph{(Initial law.)}  For \(\nu\ge0\) with
\(x_Z=\varepsilon^2D_0\nu/\gamma<1\) and \(\nu'\ge0\) with
\(x_\parallel=2\varepsilon^2u_0^2\nu'<1\),
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 \int_{\mathbb R}
 \mathcal N\!\Bigl(y-z_0;\tfrac{\varepsilon^2D_0}{2\gamma}\Bigr)
 \e^{\nu(y-\bar z)^2}\,\dd y
 &=(1-x_Z)^{-1/2}
 \exp\!\left[\frac{\nu(z_0-\bar z)^2}{1-x_Z}\right],\\
 \int_{\mathbb R}
 \mathcal N(y-r_{\parallel,0};\varepsilon^2u_0^2)\,
 \e^{\nu'y^2}\,\dd y
 &=(1-x_\parallel)^{-1/2}
 \exp\!\left[\frac{\nu'r_{\parallel,0}^2}{1-x_\parallel}\right],
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:exactmfull-b0-initlaw}
\end{equation}
exact Gaussian identities; the wrapped transverse initial factor integrates
to one against the penalty-free dominating image sums.
\item[(d)] \emph{(Budget bookkeeping.)}  Define
\begin{equation}
 \begin{gathered}
 x_V=2\varepsilon^2\rho^2\Omega_Z,
 \qquad
 x_{Z,0}=\frac{\varepsilon^2D_0\Omega_Z}{\gamma},
 \qquad
 x_{\parallel,0}=2\varepsilon^2u_0^2\Omega_\parallel,\\
 x_{\max}=\max\bigl(x_V,\,x_{Z,0},\,x_{\parallel,0}\bigr),
 \end{gathered}
 \label{eq:exactmfull-b0-xmax}
\end{equation}
and assume \(x_{\max}<1/2\).  (\(x_{\max}\) is the maximum over \emph{all}
complete-square arguments: for parameter sets with
\(D_0/(2\gamma)>\rho^2\) the initial-law argument \(x_{Z,0}\) exceeds the
node argument \(x_V\).  These scalars, and the node arguments
\(x_k=2\varepsilon^2\rho^2\nu_k\) in the proof below, are complete-square
arguments and are not to be confused with the configuration points
\(x_0,\ldots,x_{n+1}\) of Proposition~\ref{prop:exactmfull-b0-chain}.)  Define
\(\hat r=\max(a,|r_{\parallel,0}|)\) and set
\begin{equation}
 \Pi
 =\frac{\Omega_Z\hat y^{\,2}+\Omega_\parallel\hat r^{\,2}}{1-x_{\max}}
 +\frac{x_V+x_{Z,0}+x_{\parallel,0}}{2(1-x_{\max})},
 \qquad
 \hat y=\max\bigl(|z_0-\bar z|,\;\max_j|\mu(t_j)-\bar z|\bigr).
 \label{eq:exactmfull-b0-Pi}
\end{equation}
Then, along any chain whose leg fractions sum to one, the product of every
exponential and normalization factor generated by (a)--(c) is at most
\(\e^{\Pi}\), uniformly in the chain order and the leg configuration.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Equation~\eqref{eq:exactmfull-b0-square} and both identities in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:exactmfull-b0-initlaw} follow by completing the square in
\(y\) in the combined exponent (the second display of (c) is the first with
variance \(\varepsilon^2u_0^2\), centre \(r_{\parallel,0}\), and penalty
centre \(0\)); they are also verified symbolically in the numerics script.
Equation~\eqref{eq:exactmfull-b0-Sw} follows by applying
Eq.~\eqref{eq:exactmfull-b0-square} componentwise and bounding each centre
by \(\hat c\).  For (b), note that the minimum-image norm dominates the
longitudinal component.

(d)  Leg \(k\) of the chain carries coefficients
\(\nu_k=\omega_Z(h_k)\) and \(\nu_k'=\omega_\parallel(h_k)\), and its
penalty lands on the object at its starting point: an interior node ---
apply (a) and (b) with node argument
\(x_k=2\varepsilon^2\rho^2\nu_k\le x_V\) --- or the initial law ---
apply (c) with arguments at most \(x_{Z,0}\), \(x_{\parallel,0}\).  Every
midpoint centre obeys \((\mu_j-\bar z)^2\le\hat y^{\,2}\) and
 \((z_0-\bar z)^2\le\hat y^{\,2}\); every longitudinal centre term is at
 most \(\nu_k'a^2\le\nu_k'\hat r^{\,2}\) at interior nodes
 (\(|y_\parallel|<a\)) and, for the leg starting at the initial law, at most
 \(\nu_k'r_{\parallel,0}^2/(1-x_\parallel)
 \le\nu_k'\hat r^{\,2}/(1-x_{\max})\).
 The whole-window contact condition controls the deterministic relative
 trajectory only on \(I\); no condition on \(|r_{\parallel,0}|\) at time zero
 is used here.  By
Lemma~\ref{lem:exactmfull-b0-legs}(d),
\(\sum_k\nu_k\le\Omega_Z\) and
\(\sum_k\nu_k'\le\Omega_\parallel\), and every denominator obeys
\(1-x\ge1-x_{\max}\), so the exponential factors multiply to at most
 \(\exp[(\Omega_Z\hat y^{\,2}+\Omega_\parallel\hat r^{\,2})/(1-x_{\max})]\).  For
the normalization factors, \(\log[1/(1-x)]\le x/(1-x)\le x/(1-x_{\max})\)
gives
\(\prod_k(1-x_k)^{-1/2}
\le\exp[\sum_kx_k/(2(1-x_{\max}))]
\le\exp[x_V/(2(1-x_{\max}))]\)
(using \(\sum_kx_k=2\varepsilon^2\rho^2\sum_k\nu_k\le x_V\)), and the
two initial-law factors contribute at most
\(\exp[(x_{Z,0}+x_{\parallel,0})/(2(1-x_{\max}))]\).  Multiplying proves
the claim.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Flat Dyson chain bound at complex time]
\label{prop:exactmfull-b0-chain}
Fix \(t\in I\), \(\lambda\in(0,1)\), and \(0<r_0\le\tau/2\) such that
\(x_{\max}<1/2\) [Eq.~\eqref{eq:exactmfull-b0-xmax}].  Then
\(z\mapsto\mathcal R_B(z)\) is analytic on
\(\{\operatorname{Re}z>0\}\), and on the closed disk
\(D_t=\{|z-t|\le r_0\}\),
\begin{equation}
 |\mathcal R_B(z)|
 \le\hat\kappa v_\infty\e^{\Pi}
 \bigl(\e^{Bv_{\rm eff}|z|}-1\bigr),
 \qquad
 v_{\rm eff}=\hat\kappa(1+\delta_v)v_\infty,
 \label{eq:exactmfull-b0-remainderbound}
\end{equation}
with \(\hat\kappa\), \(\Pi\), \(\delta_v\) from
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:exactmfull-b0-kappahat}, \eqref{eq:exactmfull-b0-Pi},
\eqref{eq:exactmfull-b0-deltav}.  In particular
\begin{equation}
 \sup_{t\in I}\ \sup_{|z-t|\le r_0}|\mathcal R_B(z)|
 \le E_{\rm c}(B)
 =\hat\kappa v_\infty\e^{\Pi}
 \bigl[\e^{Bv_{\rm eff}(T+r_0)}-1\bigr].
 \label{eq:exactmfull-b0-Ec}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Step 1: analyticity and the Dyson series (the only, qualitative, use
of the weighted space).}
By Proposition~\ref{prop:supp-analytic}, in the self-adjoint representation
\(q=\pi u\) the free generator is nonpositive self-adjoint on
\(L^2(\pi\dd x)\) and \(-BM_{V_{w,\varepsilon}}\) is a bounded self-adjoint
perturbation, so spectral calculus defines both semigroups as analytic
operator families on \(\{\operatorname{Re}z>0\}\); conjugating by the
unitary multiplication by \(\pi\) gives analytic families on
\(X_\pi=L^2(\pi^{-1}\dd x)\).  Since \(q_0\in X_\pi\) [the strict variance
inequalities in Eqs.~\eqref{eq:exactmfull-midpoint-initial}--%
\eqref{eq:exactmfull-relative-perp-initial}] and
\(V_{w,\varepsilon}\in L^2(\pi\dd x)\cap L^\infty\), the pairing
\eqref{eq:exactmfull-b0-remainder} is continuous and
\(z\mapsto\mathcal R_B(z)\) is analytic on the right half-plane.  The
norm-convergent Dyson--Phillips series [Eq.~\eqref{eq:supp-dyson},
conjugated back from the self-adjoint representation] gives, after pairing,
\begin{equation}
 \begin{gathered}
 \mathcal R_B(z)
 =-\sum_{n\ge1}(-Bz)^n T_n(z),
 \qquad
 T_n(z)=\int_{\Delta_n}\mathcal T_n(z;\bm s)\,\dd\bm s,\\
 \mathcal T_n(z;\bm s)
 =\bigl\langle V_{w,\varepsilon},\,
 \e^{z(1-s_1)\Lfree}V_{w,\varepsilon}
 \e^{z(s_1-s_2)\Lfree}V_{w,\varepsilon}\cdots V_{w,\varepsilon}
 \e^{zs_n\Lfree}q_0\bigr\rangle,
 \end{gathered}
 \label{eq:exactmfull-b0-dyson}
\end{equation}
with \(\Delta_n=\{1\ge s_1\ge\cdots\ge s_n\ge0\}\),
\(|\Delta_n|=1/n!\).  No quantitative constant of the \(X_\pi\)
theory --- neither \(\|q_0\|_X\) nor \(v_*\), \(\kappa_\pi\) --- enters any
estimate below.

\emph{Step 2: flat form, complex continuation, and the identity bridge.}
Fix \(n\) and \(\bm s\) in the interior of \(\Delta_n\) (the boundary is
Lebesgue-null and does not affect \(T_n\)).  Write
\(h_0=1-s_1,\,h_1=s_1-s_2,\ldots,h_n=s_n\) (all positive,
\(\sum_kh_k=1\)), \(\zeta_k=zh_k\), and define the flat chain integral
\begin{equation}
 \begin{split}
 C_n(z;\bm s)
 ={}&\idotsint
 V(x_{n+1})\,p_{\zeta_0}(x_{n+1}|x_n)\,V(x_n)\,
 p_{\zeta_1}(x_n|x_{n-1})\times\\
 &\qquad\cdots\times V(x_1)\,
 p_{\zeta_n}(x_1|x_0)\,q_0(x_0)\,
 \dd x_0\cdots\dd x_{n+1},
 \end{split}
 \label{eq:exactmfull-b0-chainint}
\end{equation}
with \(V=V_{w,\varepsilon}\) and the complex-leg kernels of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:exactmfull-b0-kernels}.  We claim
\(\mathcal T_n(z;\bm s)=C_n(z;\bm s)\) on \(D_t\); note
\(D_t\subset\{\operatorname{Re}z\ge\tau-r_0\ge\tau/2>0\}\).
(i)~For real \(z=t'\in(t-r_0,t+r_0)\subset(0,\infty)\) all kernels are
positive, \(V\ge0\), \(q_0\ge0\); the operator composition in
\(\mathcal T_n\) coincides with the iterated kernel integral by the Markov
property of the free semigroup, and Tonelli's theorem identifies the
iterated integral with Eq.~\eqref{eq:exactmfull-b0-chainint}, the
\((n+1)\)-point free exposure correlation
\(\mathbb E_0[V(X_{t's_n})\cdots V(X_{t's_1})V(X_{t'})]\).
(ii)~For every \(z\in D_t\), Lemmas
\ref{lem:exactmfull-b0-domination}--\ref{lem:exactmfull-b0-absorption}
dominate the integrand of Eq.~\eqref{eq:exactmfull-b0-chainint} in absolute
value by a \(z\)-independent integrable function (for the fixed leg
fractions \(h_k\), every dominating Gaussian and image sum of
Lemma~\ref{lem:exactmfull-b0-domination} has variance bounded below on
\(D_t\) by Lemma~\ref{lem:exactmfull-b0-legs}(a), hence is bounded by a
constant; the per-leg penalties are at most \(\Omega_Zh_k\le\Omega_Z\) and
\(\Omega_\parallel h_k\le\Omega_\parallel\) by
Lemma~\ref{lem:exactmfull-b0-legs}(d); so the integrand is dominated by
\(C\prod_{k=1}^{n+1}V(x_k)\prod_{k=0}^{n}\e^{\Omega_Z(x_{k,Z}-\bar z)^2
+\Omega_\parallel x_{k,\parallel}^2}\,q_0(x_0)\), which is
\(z\)-independent and integrable because
\(x_V,x_{Z,0},x_{\parallel,0}<1\), the contact indicator bounds
\(|x_{k,\parallel}|<a\), and the transverse torus is compact); the integral
therefore converges absolutely, uniformly on
\(D_t\), and \(C_n(\cdot;\bm s)\) is continuous on \(D_t\) by dominated
convergence.
(iii)~\(C_n(\cdot;\bm s)\) is analytic on the interior of \(D_t\): for
every fixed configuration \((x_0,\ldots,x_{n+1})\) the integrand is
analytic in \(z\) there (each block kernel composes the entire functions
\(\e^{-\gamma\zeta}\) and \(v(\zeta)\) with \(\mathcal N\), well defined
because \(\operatorname{Re}v(\zeta_k)>0\) by
Lemma~\ref{lem:exactmfull-b0-legs}(a); the transverse image sums converge
locally uniformly by the same Gaussian domination); it is jointly
measurable; and it is dominated as in (ii).  Hence, for every closed
triangular contour \(\mathcal C\subset\operatorname{int}D_t\), Fubini's
theorem applies to the double integral
\(\oint_{\mathcal C}C_n(z;\bm s)\,\dd z\) (contour of finite length,
majorant independent of \(z\)) and gives
\begin{equation*}
 \oint_{\mathcal C}C_n(z;\bm s)\,\dd z
 =\idotsint
 \Bigl[\oint_{\mathcal C}
 V(x_{n+1})\,p_{\zeta_0}(x_{n+1}|x_n)\cdots
 p_{\zeta_n}(x_1|x_0)\,q_0(x_0)\,\dd z\Bigr]
 \dd x_0\cdots\dd x_{n+1}
 =0
\end{equation*}
by Cauchy's theorem pointwise in the configuration; Morera's theorem then
yields analyticity.
(iv)~\(\mathcal T_n(\cdot;\bm s)\) is analytic on
\(\operatorname{int}D_t\) as well: the semigroup families are analytic, the
composition is a finite product, and the pairing is continuous (Step~1).
By (i), \(\mathcal T_n\) and \(C_n\) agree on the real segment
\((t-r_0,t+r_0)\), which has an accumulation point in
\(\operatorname{int}D_t\); the identity theorem gives
\(\mathcal T_n=C_n\) on \(\operatorname{int}D_t\), and both sides are
continuous up to the boundary circle (for \(\mathcal T_n\) because
\(D_t\) is compactly contained in the open half-plane), so the identity
holds on all of \(D_t\).

\emph{Step 3: the chain bound.}
Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:exactmfull-b0-domination} blockwise to every leg
kernel in Eq.~\eqref{eq:exactmfull-b0-chainint}:
\begin{equation*}
 |p_{\zeta_k}(x\,|\,y)|
 \le\hat\kappa\,\bar g_k\bigl(x-\mathfrak m_R(y)\bigr)\,
 \exp\!\bigl[\omega_Z(h_k)(y_Z-\bar z)^2
 +\omega_\parallel(h_k)\,y_\parallel^2\bigr],
\end{equation*}
where \(\bar g_k\) is a product of unit-mass real Gaussians and unit-cell-%
mass image sums and the constants come from
Lemma~\ref{lem:exactmfull-b0-legs}(d), (e).  Insert these dominations (all
\(V\ge0\), \(q_0\ge0\)) and integrate from \(x_{n+1}\) inward: the
\(x_{n+1}\) integral is at most \(v_\infty\) (unit mass of
\(\bar g_0\)); each \(x_k\) integral (\(k=n,\ldots,1\)) is at most
\(\sup_x[V(x)\e^{\text{penalty}_k(x)}]\le(1+\delta_v)v_\infty\) times the
exponential and normalization factors of
Lemma~\ref{lem:exactmfull-b0-absorption}(a), (b); the \(x_0\) integral is
the initial-law factor of Lemma~\ref{lem:exactmfull-b0-absorption}(c).  By
Lemma~\ref{lem:exactmfull-b0-absorption}(d) the product of all exponential
and normalization factors is at most \(\e^{\Pi}\), uniformly in \(n\) and
\(\bm s\).  Hence
\begin{equation}
 |C_n(z;\bm s)|
 \le\hat\kappa^{\,n+1}(1+\delta_v)^n v_\infty^{\,n+1}\,\e^{\Pi}
 \qquad(z\in D_t,\ \bm s\in\Delta_n).
 \label{eq:exactmfull-b0-chainbound}
\end{equation}

\emph{Step 4: summation.}
By Step~2 and Eq.~\eqref{eq:exactmfull-b0-chainbound},
\(|T_n(z)|\le|\Delta_n|\sup_{\bm s}|C_n(z;\bm s)|
\le(1/n!)\,\hat\kappa v_\infty\e^{\Pi}
[\hat\kappa(1+\delta_v)v_\infty]^n\), so
Eq.~\eqref{eq:exactmfull-b0-dyson} gives, for
\(z\in D_t\),
\begin{equation*}
 |\mathcal R_B(z)|
 \le\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{(B|z|)^n}{n!}\,
 \hat\kappa v_\infty\e^{\Pi}
 \bigl[\hat\kappa(1+\delta_v)v_\infty\bigr]^n
 =\hat\kappa v_\infty\e^{\Pi}
 \bigl(\e^{Bv_{\rm eff}|z|}-1\bigr).
\end{equation*}
Since \(|z|\le t+r_0\le T+r_0\) on \(D_t\), taking suprema proves
Eq.~\eqref{eq:exactmfull-b0-Ec}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Fixed-allocation sufficient budget at a fixed noise
scale]
\label{prop:exactmfull-b0}
Adopt the setting above.  Fix \(\varepsilon>0\) such that the free-exposure
clock \(G=G_{\varepsilon,w}\) of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:exactmfull-factorization} has exactly \(m\) nondegenerate
maxima and \(m-1\) nondegenerate minima on \(I=[\tau,T]\), ordered
alternately, and no stationary endpoint
(Theorem~\ref{thm:exactmfull-doi} furnishes this for every
\(0<\varepsilon<\varepsilon_0\); a direct verification at one concrete
\(\varepsilon\) serves equally).  Let \(t_1^*<\cdots<t_{2m-1}^*\) denote
the stationary points, and let
\(U_1,\ldots,U_{2m-1}\subset(\tau,T)\) be any pairwise disjoint closed
root tubes with \(t_i^*\in\operatorname{int}U_i\), on each of which
\(G''\) keeps one fixed sign.  Define the margin inventory
\begin{equation}
 \mu_2=\min_{1\le i\le2m-1}\,\min_{U_i}|G''|>0,
 \qquad
 \mu_1=\min_{I\setminus\bigcup_i\operatorname{int}U_i}|G'|>0,
 \label{eq:exactmfull-b0-margins}
\end{equation}
so that \(\mu_1\) in particular bounds both endpoint slopes, since
\(\tau,T\notin\bigcup_i\operatorname{int}U_i\).  (Certified positive lower
bounds may be used in place of the two minima: the threshold below is
monotone increasing in both.)  Fix \(\lambda=1/10\) and the Cauchy radius
\begin{equation}
 r_0=\frac{\varepsilon}{\gamma\hat y}
 \sqrt{\frac{D_0\tau}{2}},
 \qquad
 \hat y=\max\bigl(|z_0-\bar z|,\;\max_j|\mu(t_j)-\bar z|\bigr),
 \label{eq:exactmfull-b0-r0}
\end{equation}
and assume the two purely geometric radius conditions
\begin{equation}
 \text{(R1)}\quad r_0\le\frac\tau2,
 \qquad\qquad
 \text{(R2)}\quad x_{\max}<\frac12,
 \label{eq:exactmfull-b0-radius}
\end{equation}
with \(t_\vartheta\), \(\Omega_Z\), \(\Omega_\parallel\), \(\hat\kappa\)
from Eqs.~\eqref{eq:exactmfull-b0-budget}--%
\eqref{eq:exactmfull-b0-kappahat} and \(x_V\), \(x_{Z,0}\),
\(x_{\parallel,0}\), \(x_{\max}\), \(\Pi\), \(\delta_v\) from
Lemma~\ref{lem:exactmfull-b0-absorption} --- all explicit functions of the
fixed data.  Set
\begin{equation}
 \hat M=\min\!\left(r_0\mu_1,\ \frac{r_0^2\mu_2}{2}\right),
 \qquad
 B_{\rm cert}(\varepsilon;w,\{U_i\})
 =\frac{\log\!\left(1+\dfrac{\hat M}{\hat\kappa v_\infty\e^{\Pi}}\right)}
 {\hat\kappa(1+\delta_v)v_\infty\,(T+r_0)}.
 \label{eq:exactmfull-b0-threshold}
\end{equation}
Then, for every
\(0<B<B_{\rm cert}(\varepsilon;w,\{U_i\})\), the budget-rescaled
Doi density \(\mathcal F_{B,\varepsilon,w}=f_{B,\varepsilon,w}/B\) of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:exactmfull-rescaled-density} has exactly \(m\) nondegenerate
maxima and \(m-1\) nondegenerate minima on \(I\), ordered alternately, and
no stationary endpoint; multiplication by \(B\) changes no stationary
point, so the same holds for the physical density
\(f_{B,\varepsilon,w}\).  In particular
\(B_{\rm top}(\varepsilon;\{w\})
\ge B_{\rm cert}(\varepsilon;w,\{U_i\})\) for every admissible choice of
root tubes.  No weighted-space constant enters: \(v_*\),
\(\kappa_\pi\), and \(\|q_0\|_X\) do not appear.
\end{proposition}

The closed form Eq.~\eqref{eq:exactmfull-b0-r0} is chosen for statement
cleanliness, not optimality: it makes the leading penalty term manifestly
\(O(1)\) uniformly in \(\varepsilon\), namely
\(\Omega_Z\hat y^{\,2}=(1+t_\vartheta^2/\lambda)\,\tau/(4(\tau-r_0))\); any
\(r_0\) and \(\lambda\) satisfying (R1)--(R2) are admissible in the proof,
and per-case optimization moves the threshold only by an \(O(1)\) factor
(Remark~\ref{rem:exactmfull-b0-anchor}).

\begin{proof}
\emph{Step 1: jet bound at the real control point, weighted-norm-free.}
Under (R1) every closed disk \(D_t=\{|z-t|\le r_0\}\), \(t\in I\), lies in
the open right half-plane, where \(G-\mathcal F\) extends to the analytic
function \(\mathcal R_B\)
(Proposition~\ref{prop:exactmfull-b0-chain}).  Cauchy's estimate on
\(D_t\) together with Eq.~\eqref{eq:exactmfull-b0-Ec} gives
\begin{equation}
 \sup_{t\in I}
 \bigl|\partial_t^r(\mathcal F_{B,\varepsilon,w}-G)\bigr|
 \le r!\,r_0^{-r}\,E_{\rm c}(B),
 \qquad r=1,2.
 \label{eq:exactmfull-b0-jet}
\end{equation}
Since \(V_{w,\varepsilon}=K_{B,w,\varepsilon}/B\) is independent of \(B\)
[Eq.~\eqref{eq:exactmfull-killing}], none of \(\hat\kappa\), \(v_\infty\),
\(\delta_v\), \(\Pi\) depends on \(B\), so \(E_{\rm c}\) is a continuous,
strictly increasing function of \(B\) with \(E_{\rm c}(0)=0\).

\emph{Step 2: the margins suffice.}
Suppose
\begin{equation}
 r_0^{-1}E_{\rm c}(B)<\mu_1
 \qquad\text{and}\qquad
 2r_0^{-2}E_{\rm c}(B)<\mu_2,
 \label{eq:exactmfull-b0-conditions}
\end{equation}
which is equivalent to \(E_{\rm c}(B)<\hat M\).  Write
\(\mathcal F=\mathcal F_{B,\varepsilon,w}\).  The complement
\(I\setminus\bigcup_i\operatorname{int}U_i\) is a union of \(2m\) closed
intervals on each of which \(G'\) is continuous and nonvanishing, with the
alternating sign pattern \(+,-,+,\ldots,-\) forced by the signature
hypothesis: the first stationary point is a maximum, the types alternate,
and no endpoint is stationary.  By the \(r=1\) case of
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:exactmfull-b0-jet}--\eqref{eq:exactmfull-b0-conditions} and
\(|G'|\ge\mu_1\) there, \(\mathcal F'\) keeps the fixed sign of \(G'\) on
each complement interval; in particular \(\mathcal F'\) has no complement
zero and neither endpoint is stationary.  The two boundary points of each
tube \(U_i\) belong to the closed complement, so \(\mathcal F'\) carries
opposite signs there.  On \(U_i\), the \(r=2\) case gives
\(|\mathcal F''-G''|<\mu_2\le|G''|\), so \(\mathcal F''\) keeps the fixed
sign of \(G''\) and is nonvanishing on \(U_i\); hence \(\mathcal F'\) is
strictly monotone on \(U_i\) and, having opposite boundary signs, vanishes
exactly once there, at a point where \(\mathcal F''\ne0\).  That zero is a
nondegenerate maximum where \(G''<0\) and a nondegenerate minimum where
\(G''>0\).  Summing over the \(2m-1\) tubes yields exactly \(m\)
nondegenerate maxima and \(m-1\) nondegenerate minima, alternating with the
tubes, and no other stationary point on \(I\).  Multiplication by \(B>0\)
changes no stationary point, so \(f_{B,\varepsilon,w}\) inherits the
signature.

\emph{Step 3: inversion.}
\(E_{\rm c}(B)<\hat M\) holds if and only if
\(\e^{Bv_{\rm eff}(T+r_0)}-1<\hat M/(\hat\kappa v_\infty\e^{\Pi})\), that
is, if and only if
\(B<B_{\rm cert}(\varepsilon;w,\{U_i\})\).  By strict
monotonicity of \(E_{\rm c}\),
Eq.~\eqref{eq:exactmfull-b0-conditions} holds for every
\(0<B<B_{\rm cert}(\varepsilon;w,\{U_i\})\), and Step~2 applies.
\end{proof}

For the concrete slab design, the only norm entering
Proposition~\ref{prop:exactmfull-b0} has a closed Gaussian form.  First,
for \(m=2\), every \(z\in\mathbb R\) lies at distance at least
\(\Delta_{\rm phys}/2\) from one of the two slab centres
[Eq.~\eqref{eq:exactmfull-slabs}], whence
\begin{equation}
 v_\infty\le
 \frac{\bigl(\max_jw_j\bigr)
       \bigl[1+\e^{-\Delta_{\rm phys}^2/(8\varepsilon^2\rho^2)}\bigr]}
      {\sqrt{2\pi}\,\varepsilon\rho\,W^{d-1}},
 \qquad
 \Delta_{\rm phys}=|\mu(t_1)-\mu(t_2)|;
 \label{eq:exactmfull-b0-vinf}
\end{equation}
for general \(m\), the normalization \(\sum_jw_j=1\) gives the cruder
\(v_\infty\le(\sqrt{2\pi}\,\varepsilon\rho\,W^{d-1})^{-1}\), and a
rigorously enclosed supremum of the mixture may be used in place of either
bound (the threshold is monotone decreasing in \(v_\infty\), so any upper
bound is safe).

\begin{remark}[The weighted-norm route, retained for comparison]
\label{rem:exactmfull-b0-baseline}
The first quantitative route --- the main proposition of earlier versions
of this subsection --- applies Theorem~\ref{thm:mixed-jet} with control
multi-index \(\alpha=0\) and time orders \(r\in\{1,2\}\).  For the
singleton family the affine chart preceding Eq.~\eqref{eq:supp-tube} may be
taken constant (\(P=0\)), so the complex-tube suprema of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:supp-free-exposure} equal the real-point norms exactly, while
\(\alpha!=1\) and \(\delta^{-|\alpha|}=1\); the singleton chart also makes
the \(\alpha=0\), \(\delta\)-free reduction of the mixed-jet theorem
exact, not merely proof-mined.  Equation~\eqref{eq:supp-mixed-bound} then
reads
\begin{equation}
 \sup_{t\in I}
 \bigl|\partial_t^r(\mathcal F_{B,\varepsilon,w}-G)\bigr|
 \le r!\left(\frac2\tau\right)^{\!r}E_{\rm wt}(B),
 \qquad
 E_{\rm wt}(B)=v_*\kappa_\pi
 \left[\e^{\frac32Bv_\infty T}-1\right]\|q_0\|_X,
 \label{eq:exactmfull-b0-baseline-jet}
\end{equation}
with \(\kappa_\pi=1\) on \(\Qinf\),
\(v_*=\|V_{w,\varepsilon}\|_{L^2(\pi\dd x)}\), and
\(\|q_0\|_X=\|q_0\|_{L^2(\pi^{-1}\dd x)}\), all evaluated at the real
control point.  Steps~2 and 3 of the proof of
Proposition~\ref{prop:exactmfull-b0} apply verbatim with
\((E_{\rm c},r_0)\) replaced by \((E_{\rm wt},\tau/2)\), giving the
weighted-norm threshold
\begin{equation}
 B_{\rm top}(\varepsilon;\{w\})\ \ge\
 B_{\rm cert}^{\rm wt}(\varepsilon;w,\{U_i\})
 =\frac{2}{3v_\infty T}
  \log\!\left(1+\frac{M}{v_*\kappa_\pi\|q_0\|_X}\right),
 \qquad
 M=\min\!\left(\frac{\tau\mu_1}{2},\ \frac{\tau^2\mu_2}{8}\right).
 \label{eq:exactmfull-b0-baseline-threshold}
\end{equation}
For the slab design the two weighted norms also have closed Gaussian
forms.  Matching Eqs.~\eqref{eq:exactmfull-midpoint-sde}--%
\eqref{eq:exactmfull-relative-perp-sde} to the forward operator
Eq.~\eqref{eq:supp-free-generator} identifies \(D=\varepsilon^2D_0\), so
the reversible density Eq.~\eqref{eq:supp-pi} factorizes as
\(\pi=\pi_Z\otimes\pi_\parallel\otimes\pi_\perp\) with
\(\pi_Z=\mathcal N(\,\cdot-\bar z;\varepsilon^2D_0/(2\gamma))\),
\(\pi_\parallel=\mathcal N(\,\cdot\,;2\varepsilon^2D_0/\gamma)\), and
\(\pi_\perp\) the normalized uniform torus factor of density
\(W^{-(d-1)}\).  The three-Gaussian product formula applied to
\(\int(\sum_jw_j\phi_{j,\varepsilon})^2\pi_Z\,\dd z\) gives, exactly,
\begin{equation}
 v_*^2=W^{-2(d-1)}I_zI_r,
 \qquad
 I_z=\sum_{j,k=1}^{m}w_jw_k\,
 \mathcal N\!\bigl(\mu(t_j)-\mu(t_k);2\varepsilon^2\rho^2\bigr)\,
 \mathcal N\!\Bigl(\tfrac{\mu(t_j)+\mu(t_k)}{2}-\bar z;\,
 \tfrac{\varepsilon^2\rho^2}{2}+\tfrac{\varepsilon^2D_0}{2\gamma}\Bigr),
 \label{eq:exactmfull-b0-vstar}
\end{equation}
where
\(I_r=\mathbb P_{\pi_\parallel\otimes\pi_\perp}(|r|_{\rm mi}<a)\le1\)
is an explicit one-dimensional integral, since \(a<W/2\) excludes wrapped
images of the contact ball.  Because \(\operatorname{Var}Z_0\) equals the
stationary midpoint variance
[Eqs.~\eqref{eq:exactmfull-midpoint-initial} and
\eqref{eq:exactmfull-stationary-variance}], the midpoint factor obeys the
exact identity
\(\int\mathcal N(z-z_0;v)^2/\mathcal N(z-\bar z;v)\,\dd z
=\e^{(z_0-\bar z)^2/v}\) with \(v=\varepsilon^2D_0/(2\gamma)\).  Hence,
for isotropic \(\Sigma_{\perp,0}=\varsigma_\perp^2I_{d-1}\),
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 \|q_0\|_X
 &=\exp\!\left[\frac{\gamma(z_0-\bar z)^2}
                     {\varepsilon^2D_0}\right]
   \sqrt{J_\parallel J_\perp},\\
 J_\parallel
 &=\frac{s_\parallel}
 {\sqrt{v_{\parallel,0}(2s_\parallel-v_{\parallel,0})}}
 \exp\!\left[
  \frac{r_{\parallel,0}^2}{2s_\parallel-v_{\parallel,0}}
 \right],\\
 s_\parallel&=\frac{2\varepsilon^2D_0}{\gamma},\qquad
 v_{\parallel,0}=\varepsilon^2u_0^2,\\
 J_\perp
 &=\left[\frac{W}{2\sqrt{\pi}\,\varepsilon\varsigma_\perp}
 \sum_{k\in\mathbb Z}
 \e^{-k^2W^2/(4\varepsilon^2\varsigma_\perp^2)}\right]^{d-1}.
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:exactmfull-b0-q0norm}
\end{equation}
the factor \(W\) per transverse dimension arising because \(\pi_\perp\) has
density \(W^{-(d-1)}\) [Eq.~\eqref{eq:supp-pi}].  The strict hypothesis
\(u_0^2<4D_0/\gamma\) is exactly the condition
\(2s_\parallel-v_{\parallel,0}>0\) required by this Gaussian integral.

Structurally, the two thresholds differ in three places.  The denominator
of the logarithm drops from \(v_*\kappa_\pi\|q_0\|_X\) --- carrying
\(\exp[\gamma(z_0-\bar z)^2/(\varepsilon^2D_0)]\), which is
\(\e^{1601.856}\) at the matched-law anchor --- to
\(\hat\kappa v_\infty\e^{\Pi}\), which carries no exponential in
\(\varepsilon^{-1}\): it equals \(2.87\) at that anchor and, along the
fixed-geometry family, grows only polynomially, at leading order as
\(\varepsilon^{-1}\) through \(v_\infty\)
[Eq.~\eqref{eq:exactmfull-b0-vinf}; \(\widehat v_\infty=1.99\) at
\(\varepsilon=0.1\) against \(3.99\) at \(\varepsilon=0.05\)].
The Cauchy radius shrinks from
\(\tau/2\) to \(r_0\propto\varepsilon\), a polynomial loss (a factor
\(\tau/(2r_0)=20\) at the anchor inside \(\hat M\)).  The exponential-rate
denominator changes from \(\tfrac32v_\infty T\) to
\(v_{\rm eff}(T+r_0)\) (at the anchor slightly \emph{smaller}, i.e.
favourable).  The weighted norm is intrinsic to the route through
Theorem~\ref{thm:mixed-jet}: its Cauchy--Schwarz pairing must be performed
in \(X_\pi\), where the complex-time free semigroup is a bounded analytic
family, and a bounded-quotient realization pays comparably through its
similarity bound \(\kappa_\pi\).  The sharpened route touches \(q_0\) only
through mass-one integrals and the initial-law factor of
Lemma~\ref{lem:exactmfull-b0-absorption}(c) (\(\approx1.29\) at the
anchor, part of \(\e^{\Pi}\)); neither \(\|q_0\|_X\) nor
\(\|q_0\|_\infty\) ever enters.  The price of generality is reversed: the
weighted-norm route applies to any analytic positive semigroup with
\(q_0\in X_\pi\), while the sharpened route is specific to the product
Ornstein--Uhlenbeck/torus structure, the Gaussian slabs, the compactly
supported contact indicator, and the Gaussian initial law of the present
design.
\end{remark}

\emph{Operational comparison and asymptotic rate.}  The companion
off-lattice campaign returns the protocol-defined classifier crossing
\(B_{\rm op}=7.62\) at the matched \(m=2\) anchor.  The nominal sufficient
expression is about \(15\) orders of magnitude smaller.  This is a diagnostic
comparison, not evidence that \(B_{\rm op}\) estimates
\(B_{\rm top}(\varepsilon;\{w\})\).  The analytic conservatism is dominated
by the left-endpoint slope
margin \(\mu_1\) --- the window-opening factor \(\e^{-30.4}\), which any
whole-window sign-preservation argument built from a uniform absolute-error
margin pays,
since the physical depletion mechanism does not care about the tiny
endpoint slope --- together with the radius factor \(r_0\) and \(O(10)\)
bookkeeping.  The semigroup side of the ledger no longer carries a
weighted-norm exponential, and its remaining constants are explicit and
polynomially bounded in \(\varepsilon^{-1}\) (a factor of order ten at the
anchor); no optimality of these constants is claimed.  Since any
whole-window sign-preservation argument built from a uniform absolute-error
margin pays the endpoint factor, further improvement requires a different
proof topology (for instance localizing the signature argument to
sub-windows on which \(|G'|\) is not uniformly tiny, or replacing the
uniform complement margin by a degree-theoretic count) rather than sharper
constants within the same transfer estimate.  Along the fixed
anchor family, provided that, as at both evaluated points, the complement
minimum \(\mu_1\) is attained at the left endpoint \(\tau\) and the \(r=1\)
condition binds, the endpoint-slope closed form
Eq.~\eqref{eq:exactmfull-b0-endpoint-slope} gives the analytic sufficient
expression the leading rate
\begin{equation}
 \log B_{\rm cert}(\varepsilon;w,\{U_i\})
 =-\frac{c_B}{\varepsilon^2}
  +O\!\left(\log\frac1\varepsilon\right),
 \qquad
 c_B=\frac{D_L^2}{2S_*^2}=0.30376.
 \label{eq:exactmfull-b0-rate}
\end{equation}
For an asymptotic justification of this proviso in the fixed matched-law
geometries considered here, choose each \(U_i\) as the maximal connected
neighbourhood of \(t_i^*\) on which \(G''\) has its sign at \(t_i^*\) and
\(|G''|\ge|G''(t_i^*)|/2\).  The weights are fixed and positive, and the
matched contact factor tends to one, with exponentially small
time-derivative corrections, as \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\).  The peak-tube
edges remain within \(O(\varepsilon)\) of their peaks, while the
valley-tube edges approach the neighbouring peaks' inner inflection points;
their slopes therefore have only algebraic dependence on \(\varepsilon\).
The outer-tail comparison then selects the left endpoint when \(D_L>D_R\),
where \(D_R=\mu(t_m)-\mu(T)\), and the shallowest valley has
\(\log\mu_2=-\Delta_{\max}^2/(8S_*^2\varepsilon^2)+O(\log(1/\varepsilon))\),
with \(\Delta_{\max}\) the largest adjacent physical gap.  Since \(r_0\)
is proportional to \(\varepsilon\), the strict inequality
\(D_L>\max(\Delta_{\max}/2,D_R)\) makes the endpoint term \(r_0\mu_1\)
asymptotically smaller than \(r_0^2\mu_2/2\).  The inequality holds in the
anchor geometry, \(0.955>\max(0.572,0.208)\), and in the \(m=3\) geometry,
\(0.629>\max(0.495,0.122)\).  This comparison concerns these fixed
geometries and this specified tube choice, not arbitrary root tubes or a
uniform allocation family.
The evaluations at \(\varepsilon=0.1\) and \(0.05\) give the predicted
decade drop \(39.58\) and the observed nominal drop \(39.57\).  For the
\(m=3\) geometry the corresponding coefficient is \(0.13180\).  Under the
same proviso, so that the leading rate of Eq.~\eqref{eq:exactmfull-b0-rate}
persists for all sufficiently small \(\varepsilon\) (the evaluations verify
it only at \(\varepsilon=0.1\) and \(0.05\)), and since
Eq.~\eqref{eq:exactmfull-bcert-relation} is one-sided, the only conclusion
for the singleton topology threshold is
\begin{equation*}
 \liminf_{\varepsilon\downarrow0}
 \varepsilon^2\log B_{\rm top}(\varepsilon;\{w\})\ge-c_B.
\end{equation*}
No asymptotic equality is claimed for \(B_{\rm top}\), and no asymptotic
relation is claimed for the protocol-defined \(B_{\rm op}\).  The Cauchy
radius \(r_0\propto\varepsilon\) contributes only to the logarithmic
correction.  Per-case optimization of \(r_0\) and \(\lambda\) changes the
nominal expression only by an \(O(1)\) factor at the displayed points.
